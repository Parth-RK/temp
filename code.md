## UNIT 1: Diversity in Living World

### Chapter: The Living World (Class 11)

*   Life is a unique, complex organization of molecules expressing through chemical reactions which lead to growth, development, responsiveness, adaptation, and reproduction.
*   Living organisms exhibit characteristics such as growth, reproduction, metabolism, cellular organisation, and consciousness.
*   **Growth:** Defined as an increase in mass and increase in number of individuals; it is a characteristic of living organisms but not a *defining* property (non-living objects also grow by accumulation of material on surface). In living organisms, growth is from inside.
*   **Reproduction:** The production of progeny possessing features similar to parents; it is a characteristic of living organisms but not a *defining* property (mules, sterile worker bees, infertile human couples do not reproduce). Can be asexual or sexual.
*   **Metabolism:** The sum total of all the chemical reactions occurring within a living body (anabolism + catabolism). Metabolism is a *defining* characteristic of all living organisms, without exception. Isolated metabolic reactions *in vitro* are living reactions but not living things.
*   **Cellular Organisation:** The organisation of the body into cells is the *defining* feature of all living organisms.
*   **Consciousness:** The ability to sense surroundings and respond to environmental stimuli. It is the most obvious and technically complicated feature. Human beings have self-consciousness. Consciousness is a *defining* property of all living organisms.
*   **Biodiversity:** Refers to the number and types of organisms present on earth. Known and described species range between 1.7-1.8 million.
*   **Need for classification:** Essential to organise the vast diversity of life for systematic study, identification, and understanding evolutionary relationships.
*   **Systematics:** The study of relationships among different organisms and their evolutionary relationships. Includes taxonomy.
*   **Taxonomy:** The process of classification based on characteristics, involving: characterisation, identification, classification, and nomenclature.
*   **Nomenclature:** The process of giving a standard name to an organism.
*   **Binomial Nomenclature (Carolus Linnaeus):** Each name has two components: Generic name and Specific epithet.
    *   Universal rules: Generally in Latin, written in italics (or underlined separately if handwritten).
    *   Genus name starts with a capital letter.
    *   Specific epithet starts with a small letter.
    *   Author's name appears after the specific epithet in abbreviated form (e.g., *Mangifera indica* Linn.).
*   **Classification:** Grouping organisms into convenient categories (taxa) based on observable characters.
*   **Taxonomic Hierarchy:** Arrangement of taxa in successive order.
    *   Kingdom → Phylum/Division → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species.
    *   Mnemonic: Dear King Philip Came Over For Good Soup (Domain, Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species) - Domain is above Kingdom.
*   **Species:** Group of individuals with fundamental similarities, can interbreed to produce fertile offspring.
*   **Genus:** Group of related species.
*   **Family:** Group of related genera.
*   **Order:** Assemblage of families.
*   **Class:** Assemblage of orders.
*   **Phylum (animals) / Division (plants):** Assemblage of classes.
*   **Kingdom:** Highest category, assemblage of phyla/divisions.
*   **Taxonomic Aids:** Tools for identification and classification.
    *   **Herbarium:** Storehouse of dried, pressed plant specimens on sheets, labelled and arranged.
    *   **Botanical Gardens:** Collections of living plants for reference.
    *   **Museum:** Collections of preserved plant and animal specimens.
    *   **Zoological Parks (Zoos):** Wild animals kept in protected environments under human care.
    *   **Key:** Analytical tool for identification based on contrasting characters (couplets, leads).
    *   **Flora:** Contains habitat and distribution of plants in a given area.
    *   **Manuals:** Information for identifying species in an area.
    *   **Monographs:** Information on any one taxon.
    *   **Catalogues:** Lists of species.

### Chapter: Biological Classification (Class 11)

*   **Early Systems:**
    *   Aristotle: Classified plants (trees, shrubs, herbs) and animals (with/without red blood).
    *   Linnaeus: Two Kingdom system (Plantae, Animalia).
*   **Five Kingdom Classification (R.H. Whittaker, 1969):** Based on cell structure, body organisation, nutrition, reproduction, phylogeny. Kingdoms: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia.
*   **Kingdom Monera:**
    *   Includes all prokaryotes (Bacteria are sole members).
    *   Most abundant microorganisms.
    *   Shapes: Coccus (sphere), Bacillus (rod), Vibrium (comma), Spirillum (spiral).
    *   Extensive metabolic diversity (autotrophic - photo/chemo; heterotrophic - saprophytic/parasitic).
    *   **Archaebacteria:** Live in harsh habitats (Halophiles - salt, Thermoacidophiles - hot springs, Methanogens - marshy areas, ruminant guts - produce methane).
    *   **Eubacteria ('True Bacteria'):** Rigid cell wall, flagellum (if motile).
        *   *Cyanobacteria (Blue-Green Algae):* Photosynthetic autotrophs (Chlorophyll a), some fix N2 (Nostoc, Anabaena) in heterocysts.
        *   *Chemosynthetic Autotrophs:* Oxidise inorganic substances (nitrates, ammonia) for energy.
        *   *Heterotrophic Bacteria:* Most abundant; decomposers, pathogens, helpers (curd, antibiotics, N2 fixation).
*   **Kingdom Protista:**
    *   All single-celled eukaryotes.
    *   Well-defined nucleus, membrane-bound organelles.
    *   Primarily aquatic. Reproduce asexually and sexually.
    *   **Chrysophytes:** Diatoms and Golden algae (desmids). Planktonic. Diatoms: 'Chief producers' in oceans, siliceous cell walls ('soap box'), form diatomaceous earth (polishing, filtration).
    *   **Dinoflagellates:** Mostly marine, photosynthetic, various colours, cellulose plates in cell wall, two flagella. Gonyaulax causes 'red tides'.
    *   **Euglenoids:** Mostly freshwater stagnant water, protein-rich pellicle (flexible), two flagella. Mixotrophic (photosynthetic in light, heterotrophic in dark). E.g., Euglena.
    *   **Slime Moulds:** Saprophytic. Form aggregation (Plasmodium) under suitable conditions. Form spores under unfavourable conditions.
    *   **Protozoans:** Heterotrophs (predators/parasites), primitive relatives of animals. Groups: Amoeboid (Amoeba - pseudopodia), Flagellated (Trypanosoma - sleeping sickness), Ciliated (Paramecium - cilia), Sporozoans (Plasmodium - malaria; infectious spore stage).
*   **Kingdom Fungi:**
    *   Heterotrophic, cosmopolitan, prefer warm/humid places.
    *   Filamentous (except yeast - unicellular). Body consists of hyphae, network is mycelium. Cell wall: Chitin & polysaccharides.
    *   Nutrition: Saprophytic, Parasitic, Symbiotic (Lichens - with algae, Mycorrhiza - with roots).
    *   Reproduction: Vegetative (fragmentation, fission, budding), Asexual (spores - conidia, sporangiospores, zoospores), Sexual (oospore, ascospore, basidiospore; steps: Plasmogamy, Karyogamy, Meiosis in zygote).
    *   Classes:
        *   *Phycomycetes:* Aquatic, damp places, obligate parasites. Aseptate/coenocytic mycelium. Asexual spores (zoospores/aplanospores). Sexual spores (zygospores). E.g., Rhizopus, Mucor, Albugo.
        *   *Ascomycetes (Sac Fungi):* Mostly multicellular (yeast unicellular), saprophytic/parasitic/coprophilous. Septate/branched mycelium. Asexual spores (conidia). Sexual spores (ascospores in ascus/ascocarp). E.g., Aspergillus, Neurospora (genetics work), Claviceps, Yeast.
        *   *Basidiomycetes (Club Fungi):* Mushrooms, bracket fungi, puffballs. Soil, logs. Septate/branched mycelium. Asexual spores rare. Vegetative: fragmentation. Sexual spores (basidiospores on basidium/basidiocarp). E.g., Agaricus, Ustilago (smut), Puccinia (rust).
        *   *Deuteromycetes (Imperfect Fungi):* Only asexual/vegetative phases known. Reproduce by conidia. Septate/branched mycelium. Saprophytes/parasites/decomposers. E.g., Alternaria, Colletotrichum, Trichoderma.
*   **Kingdom Plantae:** Eukaryotic, chlorophyll-containing organisms (details in Unit 2/4).
*   **Kingdom Animalia:** Heterotrophic, eukaryotic, multicellular, lack cell wall (details in Unit 2).
*   **Viruses, Viroids, Lichens:**
    *   **Viruses:** Non-cellular, obligate intracellular parasites, inert outside host. Genetic material (DNA or RNA, never both). Protein coat (capsid made of capsomeres). Cause diseases (mumps, smallpox, AIDS, mosaic disease). Bacteriophages infect bacteria.
    *   **Viroids (Diener):** Smaller than viruses, free RNA, no protein coat, cause plant diseases (potato spindle tuber disease).
    *   **Lichens:** Symbiotic association (Algae/phycobiont + Fungi/mycobiont). Algae provide food, Fungi provide shelter/absorption. Good pollution indicators (sensitive to SO2).

## UNIT 2: Structural Organisation in Animals and Plants

### Chapter: Morphology of Flowering Plants (Class 11)

*   **Morphology:** Study of external features.
*   **The Root:** Underground part from radicle. Tap root system (dicots - primary root persists) vs Fibrous root system (monocots - primary root replaced). Regions: Root cap, Meristematic activity, Elongation, Maturation (root hairs).
*   **Root Modifications:** Support (Prop roots - Banyan; Stilt roots - Maize, Sugarcane), Storage (Carrot, Radish, Turnip), Respiration (Pneumatophores - Rhizophora).
*   **The Stem:** Ascending part from plumule; bears nodes, internodes, buds, leaves, flowers, fruits.
*   **Stem Modifications:** Storage (Ginger, Potato, Turmeric - underground stems), Support (Tendrils - Cucumber, Grapevines), Protection (Thorns - Citrus, Bougainvillea), Photosynthesis (Flattened stem - Opuntia; Cylindrical - Euphorbia), Spread/Vegetative propagation (Runner - Grass; Stolon - Mint; Offset - Eichhornia; Sucker - Banana, Chrysanthemum).
*   **The Leaf:** Lateral, flattened structure at node, develops from shoot apical meristem. Parts: Leaf base (may have stipules, form sheath in monocots, swollen pulvinus in legumes), Petiole, Lamina (veins/veinlets).
*   **Venation:** Arrangement of veins. Reticulate (dicots), Parallel (monocots).
*   **Types of Leaves:** Simple (undivided lamina), Compound (incisions reach midrib - Pinnate, or petiole tip - Palmate).
*   **Phyllotaxy:** Arrangement of leaves on stem. Alternate (China rose), Opposite (Calotropis), Whorled (Alstonia).
*   **Leaf Modifications:** Tendrils (Peas), Spines (Cacti), Storage (Onion, Garlic), Insectivorous (Pitcher plant, Venus flytrap).
*   **The Inflorescence:** Arrangement of flowers on floral axis. Racemose (main axis grows, acropetal flowers) vs Cymose (main axis terminates, basipetal flowers).
*   **The Flower:** Modified shoot for sexual reproduction. Whorls: Calyx (sepals), Corolla (petals), Androecium (stamens), Gynoecium (carpels). Accessory whorls (calyx, corolla) vs Essential whorls (androecium, gynoecium). Perianth (calyx & corolla indistinct, e.g., Lily). Symmetry: Actinomorphic (radial), Zygomorphic (bilateral). Position of Ovary: Hypogynous (superior ovary), Perigynous (half-inferior), Epigynous (inferior ovary).
*   **Parts of a Flower:**
    *   *Calyx:* Outermost, protective sepals (gamosepalous/polysepalous).
    *   *Corolla:* Coloured petals (gamopetalous/polypetalous).
    *   *Aestivation:* Arrangement of sepals/petals in bud (Valvate, Twisted, Imbricate, Vexillary/Papilionaceous - characteristic of Fabaceae).
    *   *Androecium:* Stamens (filament + anther). Staminode (sterile stamen). Adhesion: Epipetalous (to petals), Epiphyllous (to perianth). Cohesion: Monoadelphous (one bundle - China rose), Diadelphous (two bundles - Pea), Polyadelphous (multiple bundles - Citrus). Variation in filament length.
    *   *Gynoecium:* Carpels (stigma, style, ovary). Apocarpous (free carpels) vs Syncarpous (fused carpels). Ovary contains ovules.
    *   *Placentation:* Arrangement of ovules in ovary (Marginal - Pea; Axile - China rose, Tomato; Parietal - Mustard; Free central - Dianthus; Basal - Sunflower).
*   **The Fruit:** Mature/ripened ovary. Pericarp (fruit wall - epicarp, mesocarp, endocarp). Drupe (stony endocarp - Mango, Coconut). True vs False fruit. Simple, Aggregate, Multiple.
*   **The Seed:** Fertilised ovule. Seed coat (testa, tegmen), Embryo (embryonal axis + cotyledon(s)). Endospermic/Albuminous (retains endosperm - Wheat, Maize) vs Non-endospermic/Exalbuminous (endosperm consumed - Pea, Bean).
*   **Structure of Dicot Seed:** Seed coat, Embryo (axis + 2 cotyledons).
*   **Structure of Monocot Seed:** Seed coat (fused with pericarp), Endosperm (bulky, separated by aleurone layer), Embryo (one large scutellum/cotyledon, short axis with plumule covered by coleoptile, radicle covered by coleorhiza).
*   **Semi-technical Description & Floral Formula/Diagram:** Symbols used to represent floral characters.
*   **Description of Families (Examples):**
    *   *Fabaceae (Leguminosae - Papilionoideae):* Herbs/shrubs/trees; Root nodules; Pinnately compound leaves; Racemose inflorescence; Zygomorphic, bisexual flowers; Papilionaceous corolla (vexillary); Diadelphous stamens (9)+1; Monocarpellary superior ovary, marginal placentation; Legume fruit; Seeds non-endospermic. (e.g., Pea, Bean, Gram, Groundnut, Soyabean, Indigofera, Sunhemp).
    *   *Solanaceae ('Potato family'):* Herbs/shrubs; Alternate simple leaves; Cymose inflorescence; Actinomorphic, bisexual flowers; Persistent calyx; Gamopetalous corolla; Epipetalous stamens; Bicarpellary syncarpous superior ovary, swollen placenta, axile placentation; Berry/Capsule fruit; Seeds endospermic. (e.g., Potato, Tomato, Brinjal, Chilli, Belladonna, Tobacco).
    *   *Liliaceae ('Lily family'):* Perennial herbs, underground bulbs/corms/rhizomes; Mostly basal leaves, parallel venation; Cymose inflorescence; Actinomorphic, bisexual flowers; Perianth (6 tepals); Epiphyllous stamens (6); Tricarpellary syncarpous superior ovary, axile placentation; Capsule/Berry fruit; Seeds endospermic. (e.g., Onion, Garlic, Lily, Aloe, Asparagus, Colchicine).

### Chapter: Anatomy of Flowering Plants (Class 11)

*   **Anatomy:** Study of internal structure.
*   **Tissues:** Group of cells with common origin and function.
    *   **Meristematic Tissue:** Actively dividing cells.
        *   *Apical Meristem:* Root/Shoot tips; primary growth (length).
        *   *Intercalary Meristem:* Between mature tissues (grasses); regeneration.
        *   *Lateral Meristem:* Vascular cambium, Cork cambium; secondary growth (girth).
    *   **Permanent Tissue:** Lost division ability, specialised.
        *   *Simple:* One cell type (Parenchyma - storage/photosynthesis; Collenchyma - support growing parts; Sclerenchyma - support, fibres/sclereids).
        *   *Complex:* Multiple cell types (Xylem, Phloem).
*   **Xylem:** Water/mineral transport, support. Components: Tracheids, Vessels (main water transport in angiosperms), Xylem Fibres, Xylem Parenchyma. Protoxylem (first formed) vs Metaxylem (later formed). Endarch (stem - proto towards centre) vs Exarch (root - proto towards periphery).
*   **Phloem:** Food transport. Components (Angiosperms): Sieve Tube Elements (living, enucleate, controlled by companion cell), Companion Cells, Phloem Parenchyma, Phloem Fibres (bast fibres). Protophloem vs Metaphloem.
*   **The Tissue System:** Based on structure/location.
    *   **Epidermal Tissue System:** Epidermis (outermost layer, stomata, cuticle), Trichomes (hairs), Root hairs.
    *   **Ground Tissue System:** All tissues except epidermal/vascular. Cortex, Endodermis, Pericycle, Pith, Medullary rays, Mesophyll (leaves).
    *   **Vascular Tissue System:** Xylem and Phloem in Vascular Bundles. Radial (roots), Conjoint (stems/leaves - collateral/bicollateral), Open (dicot stem - cambium present), Closed (monocot stem/leaves - cambium absent).
*   **Anatomy of Dicotyledonous Root:** Epidermis (root hairs), Cortex (parenchyma), Endodermis (barrel-shaped, Casparian strips), Pericycle (origin of lateral roots, vascular cambium), Vascular bundles (radial, usually 2-6, xylem exarch), Pith (small/absent).
*   **Anatomy of Monocotyledonous Root:** Similar to dicot, but polyarch xylem (usually >6) and large, well-developed pith. No secondary growth.
*   **Anatomy of Dicotyledonous Stem:** Epidermis (cuticle, trichomes), Cortex (hypodermis - collenchyma, general cortex - parenchyma, endodermis - starch sheath), Pericycle, Vascular bundles (conjoint, collateral, open, arranged in a ring), Medullary rays, Pith (large, central). Vascular cambium leads to secondary growth.
*   **Anatomy of Monocotyledonous Stem:** Epidermis, Hypodermis (sclerenchyma), Ground tissue (undifferentiated parenchyma), Vascular bundles (conjoint, collateral, closed, scattered, surrounded by sclerenchymatous bundle sheath). No secondary growth.
*   **Dorsiventral (Dicot) Leaf Anatomy:** Epidermis (adaxial-upper, abaxial-lower with more stomata), Mesophyll (differentiated into Palisade parenchyma - upper, tightly packed, chloroplast rich; Spongy parenchyma - lower, loosely packed, air spaces), Vascular bundles (in veins, surrounded by bundle sheath).
*   **Isobilateral (Monocot) Leaf Anatomy:** Epidermis (stomata on both surfaces), Mesophyll (undifferentiated or poorly differentiated), Bulliform cells (in grasses - water stress response), Vascular bundles (similar size, except main veins).
*   **Secondary Growth:** Increase in girth due to lateral meristems (Vascular Cambium, Cork Cambium). Mainly in dicot stems/roots.
    *   **Vascular Cambium:** Forms between xylem/phloem (fascicular) and in medullary rays (interfascicular) → forms complete ring. Activity produces Secondary Xylem (wood) inwards, Secondary Phloem outwards. Activity higher inwards. Spring wood (early, wide vessels, lighter) vs Autumn wood (late, narrow vessels, darker) → Annual Ring. Heartwood (central, dark, non-functional, deposition of tannins/resins) vs Sapwood (peripheral, lighter, functional water transport).
    *   **Cork Cambium (Phellogen):** Develops in cortex. Produces Cork (Phellem) outwards (suberin deposition, impermeable) and Secondary Cortex (Phelloderm) inwards. Phellem + Phellogen + Phelloderm = Periderm (replaces epidermis). Bark (all tissues outside vascular cambium - includes secondary phloem, periderm). Lenticels (pores in periderm for gas exchange).
*   **Secondary Growth in Roots:** Similar process, vascular cambium originates from pericycle and tissue below phloem.

### Chapter: Structural Organisation in Animals (Class 11)

*   **Animal Tissues:** Groups of similar cells performing specific function. Four types: Epithelial, Connective, Muscular, Neural.
*   **Epithelial Tissue:** Covering/lining; cells tightly packed, little matrix; basement membrane.
    *   *Simple Epithelium:* Single layer. Squamous (flattened - diffusion boundaries like alveoli, blood vessels), Cuboidal (cube-like - ducts, nephron tubules; secretion/absorption), Columnar (tall/slender - stomach/intestine lining; secretion/absorption). Modifications: Ciliated (propels mucus/eggs - bronchioles, fallopian tubes), Glandular (secretion - goblet cells, glands).
    *   *Compound Epithelium:* Multi-layered; protection (skin, buccal cavity).
    *   *Cell Junctions:* Tight (prevent leaking), Adhering (cementing), Gap (communication, ion transfer).
*   **Connective Tissue:** Most abundant; links/supports other tissues; matrix + cells + fibres.
    *   *Loose Connective:* Cells/fibres loose in matrix (Areolar - beneath skin, support; Adipose - fat storage).
    *   *Dense Connective:* Fibres dominate (Dense Regular - parallel fibres, tendons/ligaments; Dense Irregular - irregular fibres, skin).
    *   *Specialised Connective:* Cartilage (pliable matrix, chondrocytes in lacunae), Bone (hard, calcified matrix, osteocytes in lacunae), Blood (fluid matrix/plasma, RBCs, WBCs, platelets).
*   **Muscle Tissue:** Contractility.
    *   *Skeletal:* Striated, voluntary, multinucleate, attached to bones.
    *   *Smooth:* Non-striated, involuntary, uninucleate, walls of internal organs.
    *   *Cardiac:* Striated, involuntary, branched, uninucleate, intercalated discs (communication junctions) in heart wall.
*   **Neural Tissue:** Excitability, conductivity. Neurons (structural/functional unit - cell body, dendrites, axon) and Neuroglia (support cells).
*   **Earthworm (Pheretima):** Terrestrial invertebrate; reddish-brown; metameric segmentation; dorsal blood vessel visible; prostomium, peristomium, clitellum (segments 14-16, cocoon formation). Setae (locomotion). Body wall (cuticle, epidermis, muscle layers, coelomic epithelium). Coelomate. Alimentary canal (straight tube: mouth → pharynx → oesophagus → gizzard-grinding → stomach → intestine-typhlosole for absorption → anus). Circulatory system (closed; blood glands, haemoglobin dissolved in plasma). Respiration (moist skin). Excretory organs (Nephridia: septal, integumentary, pharyngeal). Nervous system (ventral nerve cord, ganglia). Hermaphrodite (testes segments 10,11; ovaries 13), cross-fertilisation via copulation. Useful ('friend of farmers').
*   **Cockroach (Periplaneta americana):** Nocturnal omnivore; damp places. Brown/black; ~3-5 cm long. Body segmented, covered by chitinous exoskeleton; divisible into Head, Thorax, Abdomen. Head (formed by fusion of 6 segments, flexible neck; compound eyes, antennae, mouthparts - labrum, mandibles, maxillae, labium, hypopharynx). Thorax (Prothorax, Mesothorax, Metathorax; 2 pairs of wings - meso: tegmina-leathery, meta: membranous-flight; 3 pairs of legs). Abdomen (10 segments; anal cerci in both sexes, anal styles in male). Alimentary canal (Foregut: pharynx, oesophagus, crop-storage, gizzard-grinding; Midgut: gastric caecae-enzymes, absorption; Hindgut: ileum, colon, rectum). Malpighian tubules (excretion, at foregut-midgut junction). Circulatory system (open; haemolymph, haemocoel; heart - elongated tube, ostia). Respiratory system (tracheal system: spiracles, trachea, tracheoles; diffusion). Nervous system (supra-oesophageal ganglion/brain, ventral nerve cord). Sense organs (antennae, eyes, maxillary palps, anal cerci). Dioecious. Male (testes, vas deferens, ejaculatory duct, mushroom gland, phallic gland, gonapophysis). Female (ovaries, oviducts, spermatheca, colleterial glands). Ootheca formation. Paurometabolous development (nymph stages).
*   **Frog (Rana tigrina):** Amphibian (lives land/water). Poikilotherm (cold-blooded). Camouflage (mimicry). Aestivation/Hibernation. Skin (moist, smooth, glands; cutaneous respiration). Body (Head, Trunk). Mouth, nostrils, bulging eyes (nictitating membrane), tympanum. Limbs (Fore-4 digits, Hind-5 digits, webbed). Digestive system (short intestine - carnivore; mouth → buccal cavity → pharynx → oesophagus → stomach → intestine → rectum → cloaca). Respiration (skin, buccal cavity, lungs). Circulatory system (closed; 3-chambered heart - 2A, 1V; sinus venosus, conus arteriosus; double circulation; hepatic/renal portal systems; nucleated RBCs). Excretory system (kidneys, ureters, cloaca, bladder; ureotelic). Nervous system (CNS - brain/spinal cord; PNS - cranial/spinal nerves; ANS). Brain (Fore: olfactory, cerebral hemispheres, diencephalon; Mid: optic lobes; Hind: cerebellum, medulla). Sense organs (touch, taste, smell, vision, hearing-tympanum/inner ear). Dioecious. Male (testes, vasa efferentia, Bidder's canal in kidney, urinogenital duct, cloaca; vocal sacs, copulatory pad). Female (ovaries, oviducts, cloaca). External fertilisation in water. Indirect development (tadpole larva).

## UNIT 3: Cell Structure and Function

### Chapter: Cell: The Unit of Life (Class 11)

*   **Cell:** Basic structural and functional unit of life.
*   **Discovery:** Hooke (dead cells), Leeuwenhoek (living cells), Brown (nucleus).
*   **Cell Theory (Schleiden & Schwann):** All organisms composed of cells/products of cells. Modified by Virchow: *Omnis cellula-e cellula* (cells arise from pre-existing cells).
*   **Cell Overview:** Membrane, Cytoplasm, Nucleus.
*   **Prokaryotic Cells:** Bacteria, BGA, Mycoplasma, PPLO. No membrane-bound nucleus/organelles. Genetic material: naked circular DNA (Nucleoid). Ribosomes: 70S. Cell Envelope (Glycocalyx, Cell Wall, Plasma Membrane). Mesosomes (plasma membrane infoldings - respiration, DNA replication, etc.). Surface structures: Flagella, Pili, Fimbriae. Inclusion bodies (storage).
*   **Eukaryotic Cells:** Protists, Fungi, Plants, Animals. Membrane-bound nucleus & organelles. Extensive compartmentalisation.
*   **Cell Membrane:** Selectively permeable; Lipid bilayer with proteins (Fluid Mosaic Model - Singer & Nicolson). Functions: transport (passive, active, facilitated diffusion, osmosis), cell recognition.
*   **Cell Wall:** Outer rigid layer in Plants (cellulose), Fungi (chitin). Provides shape, protection, prevents bursting. Middle lamella (Ca pectate) cements plant cells.
*   **Endomembrane System:** Coordinated function: ER, Golgi, Lysosomes, Vacuoles.
    *   *Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER):* Network of tubules/cisternae. RER (ribosomes - protein synthesis/secretion), SER (no ribosomes - lipid synthesis, detoxification).
    *   *Golgi Apparatus (Complex):* Stack of flattened sacs (cisternae). Modification, sorting, packaging of materials from ER. Glycosylation site.
    *   *Lysosomes:* Membrane-bound vesicles with hydrolytic enzymes (acidic pH). Intracellular digestion ('suicide bags').
    *   *Vacuoles:* Membrane-bound (tonoplast in plants). Storage (water, sap, waste). Large central vacuole in plants (turgor). Contractile vacuole (Amoeba - osmoregulation). Food vacuole.
*   **Mitochondria:** Double membrane (outer smooth, inner folded into cristae). Matrix contains enzymes for Krebs cycle. Site of aerobic respiration, ATP synthesis ('power house'). Semi-autonomous (own circular DNA, 70S ribosomes).
*   **Plastids:** In plants, euglenoids. Double membrane. Types:
    *   *Chloroplasts:* Photosynthesis. Contain chlorophyll, carotenoids in thylakoids (stacked into grana). Stroma (fluid matrix, contains enzymes for Calvin cycle). Semi-autonomous (own circular DNA, 70S ribosomes).
    *   *Chromoplasts:* Coloured pigments (carotenoids - yellow, orange, red). In fruits, flowers.
    *   *Leucoplasts:* Colourless, storage. Amyloplasts (starch), Elaioplasts (oils/fats), Aleuroplasts (proteins).
*   **Ribosomes:** Non-membranous; protein synthesis site. Composed of rRNA + proteins. Eukaryotic cytoplasm: 80S; Prokaryotes/Mitochondria/Chloroplasts: 70S. (S=Svedberg unit).
*   **Cytoskeleton:** Network of protein filaments (Microtubules, Microfilaments, Intermediate filaments). Functions: mechanical support, motility, cell shape maintenance, intracellular transport.
*   **Cilia & Flagella:** Hair-like outgrowths; involved in movement. Core (axoneme) of microtubules in 9+2 arrangement (eukaryotes). Basal body (origin, 9+0 triplet arrangement).
*   **Centrosome & Centrioles:** In animal cells. Centrosome contains two centrioles (perpendicular, 9+0 triplet microtubule arrangement). Form basal bodies, spindle fibres during cell division.
*   **Nucleus:** Control centre; contains genetic material. Double membrane (Nuclear Envelope) with nuclear pores. Nucleoplasm contains Chromatin (DNA + histone proteins) and Nucleolus (non-membranous, rRNA synthesis site).
*   **Chromosomes:** Condensed chromatin during cell division. Parts: Centromere (primary constriction, holds chromatids), Kinetochore (disc on centromere for spindle attachment). Types based on centromere position (Metacentric, Sub-metacentric, Acrocentric, Telocentric).
*   **Microbodies:** Membrane-bound vesicles with enzymes (e.g., Peroxisomes - H2O2 metabolism, Glyoxysomes - plants, fat conversion).

### Chapter: Biomolecules (Class 11)

*   **Biomolecules:** Carbon compounds in living tissues. Analysed by acid extraction (Trichloroacetic acid). Acid-soluble pool (micromolecules, <1000 Da) vs Acid-insoluble pool (macromolecules, >1000 Da - Proteins, Nucleic acids, Polysaccharides; Lipids also found here due to membrane association).
*   **Primary & Secondary Metabolites:** Primary (direct roles in growth/reproduction - amino acids, sugars). Secondary (no direct role, ecological importance - alkaloids, pigments, toxins, drugs).
*   **Carbohydrates:** (CH2O)n; energy source, structural.
    *   *Monosaccharides:* Simple sugars (Glucose, Fructose, Galactose, Ribose).
    *   *Disaccharides:* Two monosaccharides linked by glycosidic bond (Sucrose = Glu+Fru; Lactose = Glu+Gal; Maltose = Glu+Glu).
    *   *Polysaccharides:* Long chains. Storage (Starch - plants, helical, α-glucose; Glycogen - animals, branched). Structural (Cellulose - plants, linear, β-glucose; Chitin - fungi/arthropods, N-acetyl glucosamine units). Inulin (polymer of fructose).
*   **Proteins:** Polymers of amino acids linked by peptide bonds. Heteropolymers. Diverse functions (structural, enzymes, transport, hormones, antibodies).
    *   *Amino Acids:* α-carbon with amino group, carboxyl group, H, and variable R group. 20 standard types. Zwitterionic form. Essential vs Non-essential.
    *   *Protein Structure:* Primary (sequence), Secondary (α-helix, β-pleated sheet - H-bonds), Tertiary (3D folding - various bonds), Quaternary (multiple polypeptide subunits). Denaturation (loss of structure/function). Collagen (most abundant animal protein), RuBisCO (most abundant overall).
*   **Lipids:** Water-insoluble. Energy storage, membranes, hormones.
    *   *Fatty Acids:* Carboxyl group + R chain (hydrocarbon). Saturated (no C=C bonds) vs Unsaturated (one/more C=C bonds).
    *   *Glycerol:* Simple lipid (trihydroxy propane).
    *   *Fats/Oils (Triglycerides):* Esters of fatty acids with glycerol. Oils (lower melting point, more unsaturated FAs).
    *   *Phospholipids:* Lipids with phosphate group (e.g., Lecithin). Amphipathic; main component of cell membranes.
    *   *Steroids:* Complex ring structure (e.g., Cholesterol).
*   **Nucleic Acids:** Polymers of nucleotides. Genetic material (DNA), protein synthesis (RNA).
    *   *Nucleotide:* Nitrogenous base + Pentose Sugar + Phosphate group.
    *   *Nitrogenous Bases:* Purines (Adenine-A, Guanine-G), Pyrimidines (Cytosine-C, Thymine-T in DNA, Uracil-U in RNA).
    *   *Pentose Sugar:* Deoxyribose (DNA), Ribose (RNA).
    *   *Nucleoside:* Base + Sugar.
    *   *DNA Structure:* Double helix, antiparallel strands, A=T, G≡C base pairing. B-DNA most common.
*   **Enzymes:** Biological catalysts (mostly proteins, some RNA-ribozymes). Speed up reactions by lowering activation energy. Highly specific (active site).
    *   *Mechanism:* Bind substrate at active site → Enzyme-Substrate complex → Enzyme-Product complex → Release product. Lock & Key / Induced Fit models.
    *   *Factors Affecting Activity:* Temperature (optimum), pH (optimum), Substrate Concentration (reaches Vmax when saturated), Inhibitors (Competitive - bind active site; Non-competitive - bind elsewhere).
    *   *Classification (IUBMB):* 6 classes based on reaction type (Oxidoreductases, Transferases, Hydrolases, Lyases, Isomerases, Ligases).
    *   *Cofactors:* Non-protein part required for activity. Prosthetic group (tightly bound organic), Coenzyme (loosely bound organic, often vitamin derivatives, e.g., NAD, FAD), Metal ion (activator). Apoenzyme (protein part) + Cofactor = Holoenzyme (active enzyme).

### Chapter: Cell Cycle and Cell Division (Class 11)

*   **Cell Cycle:** Sequence of events where a cell duplicates its genome, synthesises constituents, and divides. Duration varies (Human ~24 hrs, Yeast ~90 min).
*   **Phases:**
    *   **Interphase:** Phase between two M phases; cell growth & DNA replication. >95% duration.
        *   *G1 Phase (Gap 1):* Interval between mitosis & S phase. Cell metabolically active, grows.
        *   *S Phase (Synthesis):* DNA replication occurs. Amount of DNA doubles (2C→4C), chromosome number remains same (2n). Histone synthesis. Centriole duplication (animal cells).
        *   *G2 Phase (Gap 2):* Proteins synthesised for mitosis, cell growth continues.
    *   **M Phase (Mitosis Phase):** Actual cell division. Includes Karyokinesis (nuclear division) and Cytokinesis (cytoplasm division).
*   **G0 Phase (Quiescent Stage):** Cells exiting G1 enter inactive stage; metabolically active but don't proliferate unless required (e.g., heart cells, neurons).
*   **Mitosis (Equational Division):** Produces two genetically identical daughter cells. For growth, repair, asexual reproduction in multicellular organisms.
    *   *Karyokinesis Stages:*
        *   *Prophase:* Chromatin condenses → visible chromosomes (2 sister chromatids attached at centromere). Centrosomes move apart, spindle fibres form. Nuclear envelope/nucleolus disappear.
        *   *Metaphase:* Chromosomes align at metaphase plate (equator). Spindle fibres attach to kinetochores of chromosomes.
        *   *Anaphase:* Centromeres split, sister chromatids separate → move to opposite poles.
        *   *Telophase:* Chromosomes reach poles, decondense → chromatin. Nuclear envelope/nucleolus reappear. Two daughter nuclei formed.
    *   *Cytokinesis:* Division of cytoplasm. Animal cells: Cleavage furrow formation. Plant cells: Cell plate formation (starts in centre, grows outwards). Syncytium (karyokinesis without cytokinesis, e.g., liquid endosperm in coconut).
*   **Significance of Mitosis:** Growth, maintaining chromosome number, repair, asexual reproduction.
*   **Meiosis (Reductional Division):** Occurs in diploid germ cells; produces four haploid cells (gametes/spores). Involves two sequential divisions (Meiosis I & Meiosis II) but only one DNA replication.
    *   **Meiosis I:** Separates homologous chromosomes.
        *   *Prophase I:* Longest phase. Sub-stages:
            *   *Leptotene:* Chromosome condensation.
            *   *Zygotene:* Pairing of homologous chromosomes (Synapsis) → Bivalent/Tetrad formation. Synaptonemal complex develops.
            *   *Pachytene:* Crossing Over (exchange of segments between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes) occurs at recombination nodules. Enzyme: Recombinase.
            *   *Diplotene:* Synaptonemal complex dissolves. Homologous chromosomes separate except at crossover points (Chiasmata).
            *   *Diakinesis:* Terminalisation of chiasmata. Nuclear envelope/nucleolus disappear. Spindle forms.
        *   *Metaphase I:* Bivalents align at equatorial plate.
        *   *Anaphase I:* Homologous chromosomes separate (sister chromatids remain attached). Segregation.
        *   *Telophase I:* Nuclear membrane may reappear. Cytokinesis follows → Dyad of cells (haploid).
    *   **Interkinesis:** Stage between Meiosis I & II; short-lived, no DNA replication.
    *   **Meiosis II:** Separates sister chromatids (similar to mitosis).
        *   *Prophase II:* Chromosomes condense, nuclear envelope disappears.
        *   *Metaphase II:* Chromosomes align at equatorial plate.
        *   *Anaphase II:* Centromeres split, sister chromatids separate → move to opposite poles.
        *   *Telophase II:* Nuclear envelope reforms around chromosome clusters. Cytokinesis follows → Tetrad of haploid cells.
*   **Significance of Meiosis:** Formation of haploid gametes (maintains chromosome number across generations), introduces genetic variation (crossing over, independent assortment of homologous chromosomes).

## UNIT 4: Plant Physiology

### Chapter: Transport in Plants (Class 11)

*   **Means of Transport:**
    *   *Diffusion:* Passive movement down concentration gradient; slow; for gases, short distances. Affected by gradient, permeability, temperature, pressure.
    *   *Facilitated Diffusion:* Passive, uses membrane proteins (channels/carriers) for substances unable to cross membrane easily; saturable, specific, sensitive to inhibitors. Porins (large pores in outer membranes of plastids/mitochondria/bacteria). Aquaporins (water channels). Uniport, Symport, Antiport carriers.
    *   *Active Transport:* Against concentration gradient; requires energy (ATP); uses membrane pumps; saturable, specific, sensitive to inhibitors.
*   **Plant-Water Relations:**
    *   *Water Potential (Ψw):* Potential energy of water; determines water movement. Pure water Ψw = 0 (max). Water moves from higher to lower Ψw. Unit: Pascals (Pa). Ψw = Ψs + Ψp.
    *   *Solute Potential (Ψs):* Effect of dissolved solutes; always negative; lowers Ψw.
    *   *Pressure Potential (Ψp):* Hydrostatic pressure; usually positive (turgor pressure in plant cells), can be negative (tension in xylem).
    *   *Osmosis:* Diffusion of water across a semipermeable membrane from high Ψw to low Ψw. Driven by water potential gradient. Osmotic pressure (potential pressure against water entry).
    *   *Plasmolysis:* Water loss from cell in hypertonic solution → protoplast shrinks from cell wall. Turgor Pressure (Ψp) develops when cell placed in hypotonic solution, prevents bursting.
    *   *Imbibition:* Water absorption by solid colloids (seeds, wood) → swelling. Special type of diffusion.
*   **Long Distance Transport (Translocation):**
    *   *Water Absorption by Roots:* Via root hairs (increase surface area). Pathways: Apoplast (cell walls, intercellular spaces - fast) and Symplast (cytoplasm, plasmodesmata - slower, involves crossing membranes). Water forced into symplast at endodermis due to Casparian strip (suberin). Mycorrhiza helps water/mineral absorption.
    *   *Ascent of Sap (Water transport up xylem):* Driven mainly by Transpiration Pull (Cohesion-Tension-Transpiration Pull Model - Dixon & Joly). Cohesion (attraction between water molecules), Adhesion (attraction to xylem walls), Surface Tension → create continuous water column with high tensile strength. Transpiration creates negative pressure (tension) pulling water up. Root Pressure (positive pressure from root osmosis) contributes minorly (guttation).
*   **Transpiration:** Loss of water vapour from aerial parts (mainly stomata). Creates transpirational pull, cools leaf surface, transports minerals. Stomata: Pores guarded by Guard Cells. Opening/closing regulated by turgor changes in guard cells (driven by K+ movement, pH, ABA). Factors affecting: Temperature, light, humidity, wind, CO2, water status, plant factors.
*   **Uptake & Transport of Mineral Nutrients:** Absorbed mostly as ions from soil, mainly by active transport. Transported via xylem along with water (transpiration stream). Some remobilisation occurs from older to younger parts (e.g., N, P, K) via phloem; some elements immobile (Ca).
*   **Phloem Transport (Food Translocation):** From Source (usually leaves) to Sink (roots, fruits, storage organs). Bidirectional. Main food: Sucrose. Pressure Flow (Mass Flow) Hypothesis (Munch): Loading of sucrose at source (active) → lowers water potential → water enters phloem from xylem → increases hydrostatic pressure → bulk flow of sap towards sink. Unloading at sink (active/passive) → raises water potential → water leaves phloem → decreases pressure. Girdling experiment proves phloem transport.

### Chapter: Mineral Nutrition (Class 11)

*   **Methods to Study Mineral Requirements:** Hydroponics (growing plants in defined nutrient solution without soil - Julius von Sachs). Essential for identifying essential elements, deficiency symptoms.
*   **Essential Mineral Elements:** Required for normal growth/reproduction, non-replaceable, directly involved in metabolism. 17 essential elements.
    *   *Criteria for Essentiality:* (Arnon & Stout).
    *   *Macronutrients:* Required in large amounts (>10 mmole/kg dry matter). C, H, O, N, P, K, S, Ca, Mg.
    *   *Micronutrients (Trace elements):* Required in small amounts (<10 mmole/kg dry matter). Fe, Mn, Cu, Mo, Zn, B, Cl, Ni.
*   **Role of Macro- & Micro-nutrients:** (Key roles)
    *   *N:* Major constituent of proteins, nucleic acids, vitamins, hormones.
    *   *P:* Constituent of cell membranes, nucleic acids, ATP, phosphorylation reactions.
    *   *K:* Abundant in meristems; anion-cation balance, protein synthesis, stomatal movement, enzyme activation.
    *   *Ca:* Cell wall synthesis (middle lamella), spindle formation, membrane function, enzyme activation.
    *   *Mg:* Constituent of chlorophyll; activates respiratory/photosynthetic enzymes; ribosome structure.
    *   *S:* Constituent of amino acids (cysteine, methionine), vitamins (thiamine, biotin), coenzyme A.
    *   *Fe:* Required in larger amount than other micros; constituent of electron transport proteins (cytochromes, ferredoxin); essential for chlorophyll synthesis.
    *   *Mn:* Activates photosynthetic/respiratory enzymes; best defined function: water splitting in photosynthesis.
    *   *Zn:* Activates carboxylases; needed for auxin synthesis.
    *   *Cu:* Involved in redox reactions (cytochrome oxidase).
    *   *B:* Required for Ca uptake, pollen germination, cell elongation/differentiation, membrane function.
    *   *Mo:* Component of nitrogenase & nitrate reductase (N metabolism).
    *   *Cl:* Anion-cation balance, water splitting in photosynthesis (with Mn).
    *   *Ni:* Component of urease enzyme.
*   **Deficiency Symptoms:** Appear when concentration is below critical concentration. Depend on element mobility: Mobile elements (N, P, K, Mg) deficiency first appears in older leaves. Immobile elements (Ca, S) deficiency first appears in younger leaves. Symptoms: Chlorosis (yellowing - N,K,Mg,S,Fe,Mn,Zn,Mo), Necrosis (tissue death - Ca,Mg,Cu,K), Stunted growth, Premature leaf/bud fall, Inhibition of cell division (N,K,S,Mo), Delayed flowering (N,S,Mo).
*   **Toxicity of Micronutrients:** Narrow optimal range. Toxicity level = concentration reducing dry weight by 10%. Excess of one element may inhibit uptake of another (e.g., Mn toxicity induces Fe, Mg, Ca deficiency).
*   **Mechanism of Mineral Absorption:** Initial rapid uptake into apoplast (passive). Slower uptake into symplast (often active, requires energy). Influx/Efflux across membrane.
*   **Nitrogen Metabolism:** Nitrogen is essential, often limiting. Plants absorb N as NO3-, NO2-, or NH4+.
    *   **Nitrogen Cycle:** Atmospheric N2 → Biological N-fixation → NH3 → Nitrification (NH3 → NO2- → NO3-) → Assimilation by plants → Ammonification (organic N → NH3) → Denitrification (NO3- → N2).
    *   **Biological Nitrogen Fixation:** Reduction of N2 to NH3 by living organisms (prokaryotes only). Enzyme: Nitrogenase (Mo-Fe protein, O2 sensitive). Free-living (Azotobacter, Beijerinckia - aerobic; Rhodospirillum - anaerobic; Cyanobacteria - Anabaena, Nostoc). Symbiotic (Rhizobium with legumes - nodule formation; Frankia with non-legumes). Nodule formation involves chemical dialogue. Leghaemoglobin (O2 scavenger) protects nitrogenase. Reaction: N2 + 8e- + 8H+ + 16ATP → 2NH3 + H2 + 16ADP + 16Pi.
    *   **Fate of Ammonia (NH3):** Protonated to NH4+. Used to synthesise amino acids via: Reductive Amination (NH4+ + α-ketoglutarate → Glutamate) and Transamination (transfer of amino group from one AA to a keto acid, enzyme: transaminase).
    *   **Amides (Asparagine, Glutamine):** Formed from aspartic acid/glutamic acid; transport N to other parts; higher N:C ratio.

### Chapter: Photosynthesis in Higher Plants (Class 11)

*   **Photosynthesis:** Process using light energy to synthesise food (glucose) from CO2 & H2O. 6CO2 + 12H2O → C6H12O6 + 6H2O + 6O2. Anabolic, endergonic, redox process. O2 comes from H2O (van Niel, Ruben & Kamen).
*   **Site:** Chloroplasts in mesophyll cells.
*   **Pigments:** In thylakoid membranes. Chlorophyll a (main pigment, reaction centre), Chlorophyll b, Carotenoids (Xanthophylls, Carotenes) are accessory pigments (absorb light, transfer energy to Chl a, protect from photo-oxidation). Absorption spectrum vs Action spectrum.
*   **Light-Dependent Reactions (Light Phase):** In grana. Light absorption, Water splitting (photolysis), O2 release, ATP & NADPH formation.
    *   *Photosystems:* Pigment complexes. PS I (P700 - absorbs far-red light), PS II (P680 - absorbs red light). LHC (Light Harvesting Complex) = antenna pigments + reaction centre (Chl a).
    *   *Electron Transport (Z-scheme):* Non-cyclic Photophosphorylation. PS II absorbs light → electron excited → passes through electron transport chain (cytochromes) to PS I → ATP synthesis (photophosphorylation). Water splits at PS II, replacing electron, releasing O2 & H+. PS I absorbs light → electron excited → passes to NADP+ → forms NADPH. Both ATP & NADPH produced.
    *   *Cyclic Photophosphorylation:* Involves only PS I. Electron cycles back to PS I via electron transport chain. Only ATP produced. Occurs when ATP needed more, or in stroma lamellae (lack PS II, NADP reductase).
    *   *Chemiosmotic Hypothesis (ATP Synthesis):* Proton (H+) gradient builds up across thylakoid membrane (in lumen) due to: water splitting (releases H+ into lumen), H+ pumping from stroma to lumen during electron transport, NADP+ reduction (removes H+ from stroma). Protons flow back to stroma through ATP synthase (F0-F1 particle), driving ATP synthesis.
*   **Light-Independent Reactions (Biosynthetic Phase / Calvin Cycle):** In stroma. Uses ATP & NADPH to fix CO2 into sugar.
    *   *Calvin Cycle (C3 Cycle):* Occurs in all photosynthetic plants.
        *   1.  *Carboxylation:* CO2 fixed to RuBP (5C) by RuBisCO enzyme → forms unstable 6C intermediate → splits into 2 molecules of 3-PGA (3C, first stable product). RuBisCO is most abundant enzyme.
        *   2.  *Reduction:* 3-PGA reduced to Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P/Triose phosphate) using ATP & NADPH.
        *   3.  *Regeneration:* Most G3P used to regenerate RuBP using ATP. Some G3P used to synthesise glucose.
        *   *Stoichiometry:* For 1 CO2 fixed: 3 ATP, 2 NADPH used. For 1 Glucose (6 CO2): 18 ATP, 12 NADPH needed.
*   **C4 Pathway (Hatch-Slack Pathway):** Adaptation in plants of hot, dry climates (maize, sugarcane, sorghum) to minimize photorespiration & conserve water.
    *   *Kranz Anatomy:* Vascular bundles surrounded by large Bundle Sheath cells (thick walls, many chloroplasts, no grana), surrounded by Mesophyll cells.
    *   *Mechanism:* CO2 fixed in mesophyll by PEP carboxylase (enzyme with high CO2 affinity, no O2 affinity) → forms Oxaloacetic acid (OAA - 4C, first stable product). OAA converted to Malate/Aspartate → transported to bundle sheath cells. Decarboxylation in bundle sheath → releases CO2 (concentrated) for Calvin cycle (run by RuBisCO). C3 acid returns to mesophyll to regenerate PEP (requires ATP). C4 plants more efficient at high temp/light, lower CO2.
*   **Photorespiration (C2 Cycle):** In C3 plants at high O2/low CO2. RuBisCO acts as oxygenase, binds O2 to RuBP → forms 1 PGA + 1 Phosphoglycolate (2C). Phosphoglycolate metabolised in peroxisomes/mitochondria, releasing CO2. Wasteful process (no ATP/sugar synthesis, loses fixed carbon). Absent in C4 plants due to CO2 concentration in bundle sheath.
*   **Factors Affecting Photosynthesis:**
    *   *External:* Light (Intensity, Quality, Duration - affects rate until saturation), CO2 Concentration (major limiting factor, ↑ rate up to saturation, higher optimum for C4), Temperature (optimum range, different for C3/C4, affects enzymes), Water (stress closes stomata, reduces activity).
    *   *Internal:* Leaf factors (number, size, age, orientation), mesophyll cells, chloroplasts, internal CO2, chlorophyll amount.
    *   *Law of Limiting Factors (Blackman):* Rate limited by factor nearest its minimal value.

### Chapter: Respiration in Plants (Class 11)

*   **Cellular Respiration:** Breakdown of organic substrates (glucose) to release energy (ATP) for cellular activities. Occurs in all living cells. C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + Energy (ATP). Amphibolic pathway.
*   **Breathing vs Respiration:** Breathing (gaseous exchange); Respiration (cellular energy release). Plants respire (take O2, give CO2) via stomata, lenticels; no specialised respiratory organs.
*   **Types of Respiration:**
    *   *Aerobic:* Complete oxidation of glucose in presence of O2. High ATP yield. Occurs in cytoplasm & mitochondria.
    *   *Anaerobic (Fermentation):* Incomplete oxidation in absence of O2. Low ATP yield. Occurs in cytoplasm.
*   **Glycolysis (EMP Pathway):** In cytoplasm. Common first step. Glucose (6C) → 2 Pyruvic acid (3C). Partial oxidation. Net gain: 2 ATP (substrate-level phosphorylation) + 2 NADH.
*   **Fate of Pyruvic Acid:** Depends on O2 availability.
    *   *Aerobic conditions:* Enters mitochondria for Krebs cycle.
    *   *Anaerobic conditions:* Fermentation (to regenerate NAD+ for glycolysis to continue).
        *   *Lactic Acid Fermentation:* Pyruvic acid → Lactic acid (by lactate dehydrogenase). In some bacteria, animal muscle cells.
        *   *Alcoholic Fermentation:* Pyruvic acid → Ethanol + CO2. Two steps (pyruvate decarboxylase, alcohol dehydrogenase). In Yeast.
*   **Aerobic Respiration (in Mitochondria):**
    *   1.  **Link Reaction (Pyruvate Oxidation):** In mitochondrial matrix. Pyruvic acid → Acetyl CoA (2C) + CO2 + NADH. Enzyme: Pyruvate dehydrogenase complex.
    *   2.  **Krebs Cycle (Citric Acid Cycle / TCA Cycle):** In mitochondrial matrix. Acetyl CoA enters cycle, combines with Oxaloacetic acid (OAA, 4C) → Citric acid (6C). Series of oxidations/decarboxylations regenerate OAA. Per cycle (per Acetyl CoA): 2 CO2 + 3 NADH + 1 FADH2 + 1 ATP (via GTP). Cycle turns twice per glucose molecule.
    *   3.  **Electron Transport System (ETS) & Oxidative Phosphorylation:** On inner mitochondrial membrane (cristae). Electrons from NADH & FADH2 (produced in glycolysis, link reaction, Krebs cycle) pass through electron carriers (Complex I → II → III → IV). Energy released pumps H+ from matrix to intermembrane space → creates proton gradient (Proton Motive Force). O2 is final electron acceptor, forms H2O. H+ flow back to matrix via ATP Synthase (Complex V / F0-F1 particle) → drives ATP synthesis.
*   **Energy Yield:** Theoretical max: ~36-38 ATP per glucose via aerobic respiration (vs 2 ATP in fermentation). Efficiency ~40%.
*   **Amphibolic Pathway:** Respiration intermediates link to other metabolic pathways (both catabolism & anabolism). E.g., Acetyl CoA → fatty acid synthesis; Amino acids can enter cycle.
*   **Respiratory Quotient (RQ):** Ratio of volume of CO2 evolved to volume of O2 consumed. RQ = Vol CO2 / Vol O2. Depends on substrate: Carbohydrates=1.0; Fats < 1.0 (~0.7); Proteins ~0.9; Organic acids > 1.0. Anaerobic RQ = Infinite.

### Chapter: Plant Growth and Development (Class 11)

*   **Growth:** Irreversible, permanent increase in size/mass/volume of organism/part, involving increase in protoplasm. Plant growth is indeterminate (meristems), localised. Open form of growth.
*   **Measurable Aspects:** Increase in length, area, volume, cell number, fresh/dry weight.
*   **Phases of Growth:** Meristematic (cell division), Elongation (cell enlargement), Maturation (differentiation).
*   **Growth Rates:**
    *   *Arithmetic:* Constant rate increase (e.g., root elongation). Lt = L0 + rt. Linear curve.
    *   *Geometric:* Initial slow (lag), rapid exponential (log), final steady phase. Common for cells/tissues/organs. W1 = W0 * e^(rt). Sigmoid (S-shaped) curve. r = relative growth rate / efficiency index. Absolute vs Relative growth rate.
*   **Conditions for Growth:** Water (turgidity, medium), Oxygen (respiration), Nutrients (macro/micro), Temperature (optimum), Light, Gravity.
*   **Differentiation, Dedifferentiation, Redifferentiation:**
    *   *Differentiation:* Meristematic cells mature → specific structure/function.
    *   *Dedifferentiation:* Differentiated cells regain division capacity (e.g., cork cambium formation).
    *   *Redifferentiation:* Dedifferentiated cells mature again into specific types.
*   **Development:** Sequence of events in life history (Seed germination → Growth → Differentiation → Maturation → Senescence → Death). Sum of growth & differentiation. Intrinsic (genetic) & Extrinsic (environmental) control.
*   **Plasticity:** Ability of plants to follow different pathways in response to environment/life phases → different structures (e.g., Heterophylly in Cotton, Coriander, Larkspur - juvenile vs adult leaves; Buttercup - terrestrial vs aquatic leaves).
*   **Plant Growth Regulators (PGRs / Phytohormones):** Small, simple molecules influencing physiological processes. Intrinsic & Extrinsic. Broadly grouped:
    *   **Growth Promoters:** Auxins, Gibberellins (GAs), Cytokinins (CKs). Involved in cell division, enlargement, tropic growth, flowering, fruiting, seed formation.
    *   **Growth Inhibitors:** Abscisic Acid (ABA), Ethylene (Gas). Involved in dormancy, abscission, stress response. Ethylene fits either group but largely inhibitory.
*   **Discovery:** Darwin (phototropism, coleoptile tip), Kurosawa (GA, foolish seedling disease), Skoog & Miller (CK, callus proliferation), Cousins (Ethylene, ripened oranges), ABA isolated later.
*   **Physiological Effects:**
    *   *Auxins (IAA, IBA, NAA, 2,4-D):* Apical dominance, Root initiation (cuttings), Flowering (pineapple), Prevent fruit/leaf drop (early), Promote abscission (older parts), Parthenocarpy (tomato), Herbicide (2,4-D kills dicot weeds), Xylem differentiation, Cell division. Produced in shoot/root tips.
    *   *Gibberellins (GA1, GA2, GA3... >100):* Increase axis length (grapes), Elongate/improve fruit shape (apple), Delay senescence, Speed up malting (brewing), Increase sugarcane yield (internode elongation), Hasten maturity/seed production (conifers), Bolting (rosette plants).
    *   *Cytokinins (Kinetin, Zeatin):* Promote cytokinesis, new leaves, chloroplasts, lateral shoot growth, adventitious shoot formation. Overcome apical dominance. Delay leaf senescence. Synthesised where rapid division occurs (root tips, buds, young fruits).
    *   *Ethylene (Gas):* Fruit ripening (respiratory climactic), Senescence & Abscission promotion, Breaks seed/bud dormancy, Sprouting (potato tubers), Internode elongation (deep water rice), Root growth & hair formation, Flowering (pineapple, mango). Synthesised by senescing tissues, ripening fruits.
    *   *Abscisic Acid (ABA):* General inhibitor, "stress hormone". Inhibits germination, promotes stomatal closure (water stress), increases stress tolerance, seed development, maturation, dormancy. Antagonistic to GAs.
*   **Interactions:** PGRs interact synergistically or antagonistically (e.g., Apical dominance: Auxin promotes, CK inhibits; Senescence: CK delays, Ethylene/ABA promote).
*   **Photoperiodism:** Response to relative lengths of day/night (photoperiod), mainly for flowering. Site of perception: Leaves. Hormone involved (hypothetical): Florigen. Types: Short Day Plants (SDP - need photoperiod shorter than critical length), Long Day Plants (LDP - need longer), Day Neutral Plants (DNP). Phytochrome pigment involved.
*   **Vernalisation:** Promotion of flowering by low temperature treatment. Prevents premature flowering. Examples: Winter cereals, Biennials (cabbage, carrot). Site of perception: Apical meristem.
*   **Seed Dormancy:** Seed fails to germinate under favourable conditions. Causes: Hard seed coat, chemical inhibitors (ABA, phenolics), immature embryo. Breaking dormancy: Scarification, chilling, light, PGRs (GAs).
*   **Seed Germination:** Embryo resumes growth. Requires water, O2, suitable temperature.
*   **Senescence & Abscission:** Ageing process; shedding of parts. Regulated by hormones (Ethylene, ABA promote).

## UNIT 5: Human Physiology

### Chapter: Human Physiology - Digestion and Absorption (Class 11)

*   **Nutrition:** Process of obtaining and utilizing food.
*   **Human Digestive System:** Alimentary Canal + Associated Glands.
*   **Alimentary Canal:** Long tube (mouth to anus).
    *   *Mouth:* Leads to Buccal/Oral Cavity.
    *   *Buccal Cavity:* Teeth (Thecodont, Diphyodont, Heterodont - Incisors, Canines, Premolars, Molars; Formula: 2123/2123 adult), Tongue (muscular, taste buds, frenulum).
    *   *Pharynx:* Common passage for food/air. Epiglottis prevents food entry into glottis (trachea opening).
    *   *Oesophagus:* Thin tube, passes through neck, thorax, diaphragm → Stomach. Gastro-oesophageal sphincter.
    *   *Stomach:* J-shaped bag; upper left abdomen. Parts: Cardiac, Fundic, Body, Pyloric. Pyloric sphincter. Rugae (folds).
    *   *Small Intestine:* Longest part. Coiled. Regions: Duodenum (C-shaped, receives bile/pancreatic juice), Jejunum (coiled middle), Ileum (highly coiled, opens into large intestine). Villi & Microvilli increase surface area. Ileocaecal valve.
    *   *Large Intestine:* Absorbs water/minerals/drugs. Regions: Caecum (blind sac, hosts microbes, vermiform appendix arises), Colon (Ascending, Transverse, Descending, Sigmoid), Rectum (stores faeces), Anus (opening).
*   **Histology of Alimentary Canal Wall:** Four layers (outer to inner):
    *   1.  *Serosa:* Outermost, thin mesothelium + connective tissue.
    *   2.  *Muscularis:* Smooth muscle (Outer Longitudinal, Inner Circular). Oblique layer in stomach.
    *   3.  *Submucosa:* Loose connective tissue (nerves, blood/lymph vessels). Brunner's glands in duodenum submucosa.
    *   4.  *Mucosa:* Innermost lining. Forms rugae (stomach), villi (small intestine). Goblet cells (mucus). Gastric glands (stomach). Crypts of Lieberkühn (between villi). Lacteal (lymph vessel in villus).
*   **Digestive Glands:**
    *   *Salivary Glands (3 pairs):* Parotid, Submandibular/Submaxillary, Sublingual. Secrete Saliva (pH ~6.8; water, electrolytes, Salivary Amylase/Ptyalin, Lysozyme). Amylase starts starch digestion.
    *   *Liver:* Largest gland. Upper right abdomen. Lobules (structural/functional units). Hepatic cells secrete Bile (stored/concentrated in Gallbladder). Bile duct + Pancreatic duct → Hepato-pancreatic duct → opens into duodenum (sphincter of Oddi). Bile: Bile salts (emulsification of fats), Bile pigments (bilirubin, biliverdin), cholesterol, phospholipids; NO enzymes.
    *   *Pancreas:* Mixed gland (Exocrine/Endocrine). Between limbs of duodenum. Exocrine part secretes alkaline Pancreatic Juice (inactive enzymes: Trypsinogen, Chymotrypsinogen, Procarboxypeptidase; active: Amylase, Lipase, Nucleases). Trypsinogen activated by Enterokinase (from intestinal mucosa) to Trypsin. Trypsin activates other enzymes. Endocrine part (Islets of Langerhans) secretes Insulin/Glucagon.
*   **Digestion of Food:** Mechanical (chewing, churning) + Chemical (enzymes).
    *   *Mouth:* Starch → (Salivary Amylase) → Maltose.
    *   *Stomach:* Gastric juice (HCl - pH 1.8, Pepsinogen, Prorennin, Lipase). HCl kills germs, activates pepsinogen → Pepsin. Proteins → (Pepsin) → Proteoses + Peptones. Renin (infants) digests milk protein (casein). Minimal fat digestion (lipase). Food becomes Chyme.
    *   *Small Intestine:* Receives Bile, Pancreatic juice, Intestinal juice (Succus Entericus - from Crypts of Lieberkühn). Bile emulsifies fats. Pancreatic enzymes act. Intestinal juice enzymes act on partially digested food.
        *   Proteins/Peptones/Proteoses → (Trypsin/Chymotrypsin/Carboxypeptidase) → Dipeptides.
        *   Polysaccharides (Starch) → (Pancreatic Amylase) → Disaccharides.
        *   Fats → (Lipases + Bile) → Diglycerides → Monoglycerides.
        *   Nucleic acids → (Nucleases) → Nucleotides → Nucleosides.
        *   Dipeptides → (Dipeptidases) → Amino acids.
        *   Maltose → (Maltase) → Glucose + Glucose.
        *   Lactose → (Lactase) → Glucose + Galactose.
        *   Sucrose → (Sucrase) → Glucose + Fructose.
        *   Nucleotides → (Nucleotidases) → Nucleosides → (Nucleosidases) → Sugars + Bases.
        *   Di/Monoglycerides → (Lipases) → Fatty acids + Glycerol.
*   **Absorption:** End products pass through mucosa → blood/lymph. Occurs mainly in small intestine (Jejunum, Ileum).
    *   *Mechanisms:* Simple diffusion (small amounts glucose, AA, Cl-), Facilitated diffusion (glucose, AA - carrier needed), Active transport (glucose, AA, Na+ - requires energy).
    *   *Fat Absorption:* Fatty acids/glycerol insoluble → form Micelles (with bile salts) → absorbed into mucosa → re-esterified to triglycerides → coated with protein → Chylomicrons → transported into Lacteals (lymph) → eventually blood.
    *   *Absorption sites:* Mouth (drugs), Stomach (water, simple sugars, alcohol), Small Intestine (principal site - nutrients, water, minerals, vitamins), Large Intestine (water, some minerals, drugs).
*   **Assimilation:** Utilisation of absorbed substances by tissues.
*   **Egestion (Defaecation):** Elimination of undigested waste (faeces) from rectum through anus.
*   **Disorders:** Jaundice (liver affected, skin/eyes yellow - bilirubin deposition), Vomiting (ejection via mouth, reflex controlled by medulla), Diarrhoea (abnormal bowel frequency, liquidity, reduced absorption), Constipation (infrequent/difficult defecation), Indigestion (food not properly digested, feeling full; causes: inadequate enzymes, anxiety, food poisoning, overeating, spicy food). Protein-Energy Malnutrition (PEM): Kwashiorkor, Marasmus.

### Chapter: Human Physiology - Breathing and Exchange of Gases (Class 11)

*   **Breathing (Ventilation):** Process of exchanging atmospheric air with alveolar air. Respiration includes breathing, gas exchange, transport, and cellular respiration.
*   **Respiratory Organs:** Vary based on habitat/organisation level (e.g., skin, gills, tracheal tubes, lungs).
*   **Human Respiratory System:**
    *   *Conducting Part:* External Nostrils → Nasal Cavity (filters, warms, humidifies air) → Pharynx → Larynx (sound box, glottis opening covered by epiglottis) → Trachea (C-shaped cartilaginous rings) → Primary Bronchi → Secondary → Tertiary Bronchi → Bronchioles → Terminal Bronchioles. Transports air, clears foreign particles.
    *   *Exchange Part:* Respiratory Bronchioles → Alveolar Ducts → Alveoli (thin-walled, vascularised sacs). Site of actual gas diffusion.
*   **Lungs:** Paired, spongy organs in airtight thoracic cavity. Covered by double pleural membrane (pleural fluid reduces friction). Right lung (3 lobes), Left lung (2 lobes). Thoracic cavity formed by vertebral column (dorsal), sternum (ventral), ribs (lateral), diaphragm (dome-shaped, lower). Setup essential for breathing.
*   **Mechanism of Breathing:** Based on pressure gradients.
    *   *Inspiration (Active):* Diaphragm contracts (flattens). External intercostal muscles contract (lift ribs/sternum up & out). Thoracic volume increases → Pulmonary volume increases → Intra-pulmonary pressure decreases (< atmospheric) → Air moves in.
    *   *Expiration (Normally Passive):* Diaphragm & external intercostals relax. Thoracic volume decreases → Pulmonary volume decreases → Intra-pulmonary pressure increases (> atmospheric) → Air moves out.
    *   *Forced Breathing:* Uses abdominal muscles & internal intercostals for forceful expiration/inspiration.
*   **Respiratory Volumes & Capacities:** Measured by Spirometer.
    *   *Tidal Volume (TV):* Air volume in normal breath (~500 mL).
    *   *Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV):* Max additional air inhaled forcefully (~2500-3000 mL).
    *   *Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV):* Max additional air exhaled forcefully (~1000-1100 mL).
    *   *Residual Volume (RV):* Air remaining in lungs after forceful expiration (~1100-1200 mL). Cannot be measured directly by spirometry.
    *   *Inspiratory Capacity (IC):* TV + IRV.
    *   *Expiratory Capacity (EC):* TV + ERV.
    *   *Functional Residual Capacity (FRC):* ERV + RV (Air after normal expiration).
    *   *Vital Capacity (VC):* IRV + TV + ERV (Max air breathed out after max inhalation).
    *   *Total Lung Capacity (TLC):* VC + RV (Total air in lungs after max inhalation).
*   **Exchange of Gases:** By simple diffusion based on partial pressure gradients (Pressure exerted by individual gas in mixture).
    *   *Alveoli ↔ Blood:* O2 diffuses from alveoli (pO2 ~104 mmHg) to blood (pO2 ~40 mmHg). CO2 diffuses from blood (pCO2 ~45 mmHg) to alveoli (pCO2 ~40 mmHg).
    *   *Blood ↔ Tissues:* O2 diffuses from blood (pO2 ~95 mmHg) to tissues (pO2 ~40 mmHg). CO2 diffuses from tissues (pCO2 ~45 mmHg) to blood (pCO2 ~40 mmHg).
    *   *Diffusion Membrane:* Alveolar epithelium, capillary endothelium, basement substance between them. Very thin (< 1 mm). Factors favouring diffusion: Thin membrane, large surface area, pressure gradients, high solubility of CO2 (20-25x > O2).
*   **Transport of Gases:**
    *   *Oxygen:* ~97% bound to Haemoglobin (Hb) in RBCs → Oxyhaemoglobin (HbO2). ~3% dissolved in plasma. Binding is reversible, depends on pO2 (major factor), pCO2, H+, temperature. 1 Hb carries max 4 O2.
        *   *Oxygen-Dissociation Curve:* Sigmoid (S-shaped). Shows % Hb saturation vs pO2. High pO2 (alveoli) favours binding; Low pO2 (tissues) favours dissociation. Factors shifting curve Right (favours dissociation, ↓affinity): ↑pCO2, ↑H+ (↓pH - Bohr effect), ↑Temperature, ↑2,3-BPG.
    *   *Carbon Dioxide:* ~20-25% bound to Hb → Carbamino-haemoglobin (HbCO2). ~70% transported as Bicarbonate ions (HCO3-) in plasma. ~7% dissolved in plasma.
        *   *Bicarbonate Formation:* CO2 diffuses into RBCs → combines with H2O (catalysed by Carbonic Anhydrase) → H2CO3 (carbonic acid) → dissociates into H+ + HCO3-. HCO3- moves into plasma, Cl- moves into RBCs (Chloride Shift / Hamburger phenomenon). Reversed process at alveoli. Hb buffers H+. Haldane Effect: Deoxygenation of Hb increases its CO2 carrying capacity.
*   **Regulation of Respiration:**
    *   *Neural Control:* Respiratory Rhythm Centre (Medulla oblongata - controls basic rhythm). Pneumotaxic Centre (Pons - moderates/limits inspiration). Apneustic Centre (Pons - promotes inspiration).
    *   *Chemical Control:* Chemosensitive Area (adjacent to rhythm centre, sensitive to ↑CO2 & ↑H+). Peripheral Chemoreceptors (Aortic bodies, Carotid bodies - sensitive to ↑CO2, ↑H+, and significantly ↓pO2). CO2/H+ have major regulatory role; O2 has limited role.
*   **Disorders:** Asthma (inflammation/spasm of bronchi/bronchioles → wheezing), Emphysema (alveolar wall damage → ↓surface area; major cause: smoking), Occupational Respiratory Disorders (Fibrosis/inflammation due to long exposure to dust - Silicosis, Asbestosis; protective masks needed).

### Chapter: Human Physiology - Body Fluids and Circulation (Class 11)

*   **Need for Circulation:** Transport of nutrients, O2, wastes, hormones etc.
*   **Body Fluids:** Blood (main circulating fluid) and Lymph (tissue fluid).
*   **Blood:** Special connective tissue. Fluid Matrix (Plasma) + Formed Elements.
    *   *Plasma (~55%):* Straw-coloured. Water (90-92%), Proteins (6-8%: Fibrinogen - clotting, Globulins - defence/antibodies, Albumins - osmotic balance), Glucose, Amino acids, Lipids, Ions, Hormones, Clotting factors etc. Serum = Plasma minus fibrinogen/clotting factors.
    *   *Formed Elements (~45%):*
        *   *Erythrocytes (RBCs):* Most abundant. Biconcave disc shape. Anucleated in mammals. Contain Haemoglobin (red pigment, O2 transport). Lifespan ~120 days. Formed in red bone marrow (Erythropoiesis). Destroyed in spleen ('graveyard'). Count: ~5-5.5 million/mm³.
        *   *Leucocytes (WBCs):* Colourless, nucleated, amoeboid. Defence function. Count: ~6000-8000/mm³. Types:
            *   *Granulocytes:* Neutrophils (60-65%, phagocytic), Eosinophils (2-3%, resist infections, allergic reactions), Basophils (0.5-1%, secrete histamine, serotonin, heparin; inflammation).
            *   *Agranulocytes:* Lymphocytes (20-25%, B & T types, specific immunity), Monocytes (6-8%, largest, phagocytic → macrophages).
        *   *Thrombocytes (Platelets):* Cell fragments from megakaryocytes. Involved in blood clotting. Count: ~1.5-3.5 lakh/mm³.
*   **Blood Groups:**
    *   *ABO System:* Based on presence/absence of Antigens A & B on RBC surface, and Antibodies Anti-A & Anti-B in plasma. Group A (Ag A, Ab Anti-B), B (Ag B, Ab Anti-A), AB (Ag A & B, No Ab - Universal Recipient), O (No Ag, Ab Anti-A & Anti-B - Universal Donor). Important for transfusion.
    *   *Rh System:* Based on Rh antigen (similar to Rhesus monkey). Rh+ (antigen present), Rh- (absent). Important for transfusion & pregnancy. Rh Incompatibility / Erythroblastosis Fetalis: Rh- mother carrying Rh+ fetus → mother forms Anti-Rh antibodies → can affect subsequent Rh+ pregnancies (haemolysis in fetus). Prevented by administering Anti-Rh antibodies (RhoGAM) to mother post-delivery.
*   **Coagulation of Blood (Clotting):** Mechanism to prevent blood loss. Injury → Platelet aggregation, release of Thromboplastin (factor III). Cascade involving multiple Clotting Factors (inactive forms activated). Prothrombin → (Thrombokinase enzyme complex + Ca++) → Thrombin (active). Fibrinogen (soluble) → (Thrombin) → Fibrin (insoluble threads) → forms network trapping cells → Clot. Requires Ca++.
*   **Lymph (Tissue Fluid):** Fluid leaked from blood capillaries into interstitial spaces. Similar composition to plasma but fewer proteins, no RBCs, more lymphocytes. Collected by Lymphatic capillaries → Lymphatic vessels → return to blood via subclavian veins. Functions: Exchange medium, Fat absorption (via lacteals in villi), Immune response. Lymph nodes filter lymph.
*   **Circulatory Pathways:** Open (blood pumped into haemocoel, e.g., arthropods, molluscs) vs Closed (blood confined to vessels, e.g., annelids, chordates). Humans have closed system.
*   **Human Circulatory System:** Heart + Blood Vessels (Arteries, Arterioles, Capillaries, Venules, Veins) + Blood.
*   **Human Heart:** Muscular, 4-chambered (2 Atria - upper, receiving; 2 Ventricles - lower, pumping). Location: Thoracic cavity, between lungs (mediastinum), tilted left. Protected by Pericardium (double membrane with pericardial fluid). Septa separate chambers (Interatrial, Interventricular, Atrioventricular). Valves prevent backflow: Tricuspid (RA → RV), Bicuspid/Mitral (LA → LV), Semilunar (Pulmonary - RV → pulm. artery; Aortic - LV → aorta). Chordae tendineae & Papillary muscles support AV valves.
*   **Cardiac Muscle:** Striated, involuntary, branched, intercalated discs (gap junctions for rapid impulse spread).
*   **Conduction System:** Generates/conducts impulse for rhythmic contraction. Myogenic heart. Sino-atrial Node (SAN) - 'Pacemaker' in RA wall, generates impulse (~70-75/min). Atrio-ventricular Node (AVN) - RA wall near AV septum, delays impulse slightly. AV Bundle (Bundle of His) - from AVN passes through AV septum. Purkinje Fibres - spread through ventricle walls.
*   **Cardiac Cycle:** Sequence of events during one heartbeat (~0.8 sec). Joint Diastole (all chambers relaxed, AV valves open, filling) → Atrial Systole (atria contract, push blood to ventricles) → Ventricular Systole (ventricles contract; AV valves close-'LUB'; semilunar valves open, blood ejected) → Ventricular Diastole (ventricles relax; semilunar valves close-'DUB'; AV valves open).
*   **Stroke Volume (SV):** Volume pumped per ventricle per beat (~70 mL).
*   **Cardiac Output (CO):** Volume pumped per ventricle per minute. CO = SV × Heart Rate (HR). (~ 5 Litres/min at rest).
*   **Electrocardiogram (ECG):** Recording of electrical activity of heart during cardiac cycle. Standard ECG uses leads attached to limbs/chest. Waves: P wave (atrial depolarisation/contraction), QRS complex (ventricular depolarisation/contraction), T wave (ventricular repolarisation/relaxation). Useful diagnostic tool.
*   **Double Circulation:** Blood flows through heart twice per cycle. Ensures complete separation of oxygenated & deoxygenated blood. Pulmonary Circuit (Right Ventricle → Lungs → Left Atrium). Systemic Circuit (Left Ventricle → Body Tissues → Right Atrium). Unique vascular connections: Hepatic Portal System (intestine to liver), Coronary Circulation (supplies heart muscle).
*   **Regulation of Cardiac Activity:** Intrinsic (SAN). Extrinsic: Neural (Medulla oblongata - ANS: Sympathetic ↑ HR/force, Parasympathetic ↓ HR), Hormonal (Adrenal medulla hormones ↑ HR/force).
*   **Disorders:** Hypertension (High Blood Pressure: >140/90; affects vital organs), Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)/Atherosclerosis (plaque - Ca, fat, cholesterol - deposition in coronary arteries, narrows lumen), Angina (Angina Pectoris: Chest pain due to transient lack of O2 to heart muscle), Heart Failure (Heart not pumping effectively to meet body needs; 'Congestive heart failure' if lung congestion occurs), Cardiac Arrest (heart stops beating), Heart Attack (Myocardial Infarction: heart muscle damage due to inadequate blood supply).

### Chapter: Human Physiology - Excretory Products and their Elimination (Class 11)

*   **Excretion:** Elimination of metabolic wastes, primarily nitrogenous wastes. Maintains homeostasis (Osmoregulation: water/ionic balance).
*   **Nitrogenous Wastes:** Ammonia (most toxic, requires most water; Ammonotelism - aquatic invertebrates, bony fish). Urea (less toxic, requires less water; Ureotelism - mammals, amphibians, marine fish; formed in liver via Urea Cycle). Uric Acid (least toxic, least water/paste form; Uricotelism - reptiles, birds, insects, land snails).
*   **Excretory Structures in Animals:** Protonephridia/Flame cells (Platyhelminthes, Rotifers), Nephridia (Annelids), Malpighian Tubules (Insects), Antennal/Green Glands (Crustaceans), Kidneys (Vertebrates).
*   **Human Excretory System:** Pair of Kidneys, Pair of Ureters, Urinary Bladder, Urethra.
*   **Kidney Anatomy:** Bean-shaped, reddish-brown, retroperitoneal. Hilum (notch for ureter, vessels, nerves entry/exit). Internally: Outer Cortex, Inner Medulla (divided into Medullary Pyramids projecting into Calyces → Renal Pelvis → Ureter). Covered by fibrous capsule.
*   **Nephron:** Structural & functional unit (~1 million/kidney). Parts:
    *   *Renal Corpuscle (Malpighian Body):* Glomerulus (tuft of capillaries from afferent arteriole, exits via efferent arteriole) + Bowman's Capsule (double-walled cup surrounding glomerulus).
    *   *Renal Tubule:* Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT) → Loop of Henle (descending & ascending limbs) → Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT). DCTs open into Collecting Duct. Collecting ducts converge → Papillary ducts → open into renal pelvis via medullary pyramids.
*   **Types of Nephrons:** Cortical (short Loop of Henle, mainly in cortex) & Juxtamedullary (long Loop of Henle extending deep into medulla, crucial for urine concentration). Vasa Recta (capillary loop parallel to Henle's loop in juxtamedullary nephrons).
*   **Urine Formation:** Three processes in nephron:
    *   1.  **Glomerular Filtration:** Blood filtered from glomerulus into Bowman's capsule under pressure. Filtration membrane (glomerular endothelium, basement membrane, Bowman's capsule epithelium/podocytes with filtration slits). Ultrafiltration (filters plasma except proteins). Glomerular Filtrate Rate (GFR) ≈ 125 mL/min or 180 L/day.
    *   2.  **Tubular Reabsorption:** Selective movement of useful substances from filtrate back into blood (peritubular capillaries/vasa recta). ~99% filtrate reabsorbed. PCT (major site: absorbs glucose, amino acids, Na+, K+, water, HCO3-). Loop of Henle (Descending limb permeable to water, Ascending limb permeable to salts/impermeable to water). DCT (conditional Na+/water reabsorption). Collecting Duct (water, some urea reabsorption). Mechanisms: Active & Passive transport.
    *   3.  **Tubular Secretion:** Selective movement of wastes (H+, K+, ammonia, drugs) from blood into filtrate. Occurs in PCT, DCT, Collecting Duct. Important for maintaining ionic & acid-base balance.
*   **Mechanism of Urine Concentration (Countercurrent Mechanism):** Ability to produce concentrated urine (hypertonic to blood). Involves Loop of Henle & Vasa Recta acting as countercurrent multiplier & exchanger. Creates increasing osmolarity gradient in medullary interstitium (300 → 1200 mOsm/L). Gradient maintained by NaCl & Urea. Allows water reabsorption from collecting duct under ADH influence.
*   **Regulation of Kidney Function:**
    *   *Hormonal Control:*
        *   *ADH (Antidiuretic Hormone/Vasopressin):* Released from posterior pituitary (synthesised in hypothalamus). Triggered by ↑blood osmolarity/↓volume. Increases water permeability of DCT & Collecting Duct → ↑water reabsorption → concentrates urine, ↓blood osmolarity. Deficiency → Diabetes Insipidus.
        *   *RAAS (Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System):* Triggered by ↓GFR/Blood Pressure. Juxtaglomerular Apparatus (JGA - specialised cells in DCT/afferent arteriole) releases Renin. Renin converts Angiotensinogen (liver) → Angiotensin I → Angiotensin II (potent vasoconstrictor, stimulates Aldosterone release). Aldosterone (adrenal cortex) → ↑Na+ & water reabsorption in DCT → ↑Blood volume/Pressure/GFR.
        *   *ANF (Atrial Natriuretic Factor):* Released by heart atria in response to ↑Blood Pressure. Causes vasodilation, inhibits Renin/Aldosterone release → ↓Blood Pressure. Opposes RAAS.
*   **Micturition (Urination):** Release of urine from bladder. Bladder fills → stretch receptors signal CNS → conscious urge → relaxation of urethral sphincters. Voluntary control.
*   **Role of Other Organs in Excretion:** Lungs (CO2, water vapour), Liver (bile pigments, urea formation), Skin (sweat - NaCl, urea, water; sebum - sterols, waxes).
*   **Disorders:** Kidney Failure/Renal Failure (loss of function → Uremia: urea accumulation in blood). Treatment: Haemodialysis ('artificial kidney'), Kidney Transplantation. Renal Calculi (Kidney stones: insoluble crystals - oxalates etc.). Glomerulonephritis (inflammation of glomeruli).

### Chapter: Human Physiology - Locomotion and Movement (Class 11)

*   **Movement vs Locomotion:** Movement (change in posture/part position); Locomotion (movement resulting in change of location). All locomotion is movement, but not all movement is locomotion.
*   **Types of Movement:** Amoeboid (pseudopodia - macrophages, leucocytes), Ciliary (cilia - respiratory tract, fallopian tubes), Muscular (contractile tissue - limbs, jaw, tongue).
*   **Muscle Tissue:** Properties: Excitability, Contractility, Extensibility, Elasticity. Types:
    *   *Skeletal Muscle:* Attached to bones, voluntary control, striated appearance, multinucleated fibres.
    *   *Visceral (Smooth) Muscle:* Walls of internal organs (gut, vessels), involuntary, non-striated, spindle-shaped cells.
    *   *Cardiac Muscle:* Heart wall, involuntary, striated, branched fibres, intercalated discs (junctions).
*   **Skeletal Muscle Structure:** Muscle → Fascicles (bundles) → Muscle Fibres (cells) → Myofibrils → Myofilaments (Actin & Myosin).
    *   *Muscle Fibre:* Multinucleated syncytium, Sarcolemma (membrane), Sarcoplasm (cytoplasm), Sarcoplasmic Reticulum (ER, stores Ca++).
    *   *Myofibril:* Alternating dark (A-band) and light (I-band) bands → striated appearance.
        *   *A-band:* Contains Myosin (thick filament) + overlapping Actin (thin filament). H-zone (centre, myosin only). M-line (centre of H-zone).
        *   *I-band:* Contains Actin only. Z-line (bisects I-band).
    *   *Sarcomere:* Functional unit of contraction; region between two successive Z-lines.
*   **Contractile Proteins:**
    *   *Myosin (Thick Filament):* Polymerised protein. Monomer (Meromyosin) has Head (globular, actin binding site, ATPase activity) and Tail. Heads project outwards.
    *   *Actin (Thin Filament):* Two F-actin (filamentous) helically wound. Each F-actin is polymer of G-actin (globular). Associated proteins: Tropomyosin (runs along F-actin), Troponin (complex of 3 proteins, distributed on tropomyosin, masks myosin binding sites on actin at rest).
*   **Mechanism of Muscle Contraction (Sliding Filament Theory):** Contraction = shortening of sarcomere due to actin filaments sliding over myosin filaments.
    *   1.  *Signal:* Motor neuron releases Acetylcholine (ACh) at Neuromuscular Junction → Action Potential (AP) generated on sarcolemma.
    *   2.  *Excitation-Contraction Coupling:* AP spreads via T-tubules → Sarcoplasmic Reticulum releases Ca++.
    *   3.  *Activation:* Ca++ binds to Troponin → causes conformational change → Tropomyosin shifts → exposes Myosin-binding sites on Actin.
    *   4.  *Cross-Bridge Formation:* Energised Myosin head (ADP+Pi bound) binds to exposed site on Actin.
    *   5.  *Power Stroke:* Myosin head releases ADP+Pi, pivots/bends → pulls Actin filament towards M-line (centre of sarcomere). Sarcomere shortens.
    *   6.  *Cross-Bridge Detachment:* New ATP binds to Myosin head → link weakens, head detaches.
    *   7.  *Reactivation:* Myosin head hydrolyses ATP → ADP+Pi, re-energised for next cycle.
    *   *Cycle repeats* as long as Ca++ & ATP are present. Z-lines pulled closer, I-bands shorten, H-zone disappears. A-band length remains constant.
*   **Muscle Relaxation:** Nerve signal stops → Ca++ pumped back into Sarcoplasmic Reticulum (active transport) → Troponin-Tropomyosin complex covers Actin binding sites → cross-bridges detach.
*   **Muscle Fatigue:** Due to accumulation of lactic acid (anaerobic respiration). Muscle Fibre Types: Red (aerobic, myoglobin-rich, slow fatigue) vs White (anaerobic, less myoglobin, fast fatigue).
*   **Skeletal System:** Framework of bones & cartilage. Functions: Support, Protection, Movement (leverage), Blood cell formation (haemopoiesis in marrow), Mineral storage (Ca, P). 206 bones in adult human.
    *   **Axial Skeleton (80 bones):** Skull (Cranium-8, Facial-14, Hyoid-1, Ear Ossicles-6), Vertebral Column (26: C7, T12, L5, Sacrum-1 fused, Coccyx-1 fused), Sternum (1), Ribs (12 pairs: 7 true, 3 false/vertebrochondral, 2 floating).
    *   **Appendicular Skeleton (126 bones):**
        *   *Pectoral Girdle (Shoulder):* Clavicle (collar bone) + Scapula (shoulder blade).
        *   *Pelvic Girdle (Hip):* 2 Coxal bones (each fused Ilium, Ischium, Pubis).
        *   *Limbs (Forelimb - 30 bones):* Humerus, Radius & Ulna, Carpals (wrist-8), Metacarpals (palm-5), Phalanges (digits-14).
        *   *Limbs (Hindlimb - 30 bones):* Femur (thigh bone), Patella (knee cap), Tibia & Fibula, Tarsals (ankle-7), Metatarsals (sole-5), Phalanges (digits-14).
*   **Joints:** Points of contact between bones, or bone & cartilage. Classification based on structure/mobility:
    *   *Fibrous Joints:* Dense connective tissue, immovable (e.g., Sutures in skull, Gomphosis-tooth).
    *   *Cartilaginous Joints:* Cartilage connects bones, slightly movable (e.g., Intervertebral discs, Pubic symphysis).
    *   *Synovial Joints:* Freely movable. Characterised by Synovial cavity filled with synovial fluid, Articular cartilage covering bone ends, Joint capsule. Types: Ball & Socket (shoulder, hip), Hinge (elbow, knee), Pivot (atlas-axis), Gliding (carpals), Saddle (thumb carpo-metacarpal).
*   **Disorders:** Myasthenia Gravis (autoimmune, affects NMJ → fatigue, weakness), Muscular Dystrophy (genetic, progressive muscle degeneration), Tetany (low blood Ca++ → rapid muscle spasms), Arthritis (joint inflammation - Osteoarthritis, Rheumatoid arthritis, Gouty arthritis), Osteoporosis (decreased bone mass, ↑fracture risk; age/hormone related), Gout (uric acid crystal accumulation in joints).

### Chapter: Human Physiology - Neural Control and Coordination (Class 11)

*   **Coordination:** Process integrating activities of organs for harmonious function. Neural system & Endocrine system coordinate/integrate activities. Neural system provides rapid point-to-point coordination.
*   **Neural System:** Composed of Neurons (structural/functional units), Neuroglia (support cells).
*   **Neuron Structure:** Cell Body (Soma/Cyton - contains nucleus, Nissl's granules), Dendrites (short, branched processes, receive signals), Axon (long process, transmits signals away), Axon Terminal (ends in Synaptic Knobs containing neurotransmitters).
*   **Types of Neurons:** Multipolar (1 axon, many dendrites - common), Bipolar (1 axon, 1 dendrite - retina), Unipolar (cell body with 1 axon - embryonic stage).
*   **Axons:** Myelinated (covered by Myelin Sheath - formed by Schwann cells in PNS, Oligodendrocytes in CNS; insulating layer). Gaps in myelin sheath: Nodes of Ranvier. Saltatory Conduction (impulse jumps node to node, faster). Unmyelinated (no sheath, slower conduction).
*   **Nerve Impulse Generation & Conduction:** Neurons are excitable (polarised membrane).
    *   *Resting Potential:* Membrane potential difference across resting neuron membrane (~ -70mV, inside negative). Due to differential ion distribution (high Na+ outside, high K+ inside, negative proteins inside), differential permeability (more permeable to K+ than Na+ at rest), Na+-K+ Pump (actively pumps 3 Na+ out for 2 K+ in).
    *   *Action Potential (Nerve Impulse):* Rapid change in membrane potential upon stimulation. Stimulus reaches threshold → Voltage-gated Na+ channels open → Rapid Na+ influx → Depolarisation (membrane becomes positive inside, ~ +30mV). Followed by Repolarisation: Na+ channels close, Voltage-gated K+ channels open → K+ efflux → Membrane potential restored to negative (may hyperpolarise briefly). Impulse propagates along axon as wave of depolarisation. All-or-none principle.
*   **Synaptic Transmission:** Transmission of impulse from one neuron (presynaptic) to another (postsynaptic) at a Synapse.
    *   *Electrical Synapse:* Direct current flow via gap junctions. Faster, rare in humans.
    *   *Chemical Synapse:* Involves Neurotransmitters. Impulse reaches axon terminal → Voltage-gated Ca++ channels open → Ca++ influx → Synaptic vesicles fuse with presynaptic membrane → Release neurotransmitter (e.g., Acetylcholine, Noradrenaline, Dopamine, Serotonin, GABA) into Synaptic Cleft → Neurotransmitter binds to receptors on postsynaptic membrane → Opens ion channels → Causes postsynaptic potential (Excitatory-EPSP/depolarisation OR Inhibitory-IPSP/hyperpolarisation). Enzyme degrades/reuptake removes neurotransmitter.
*   **Human Nervous System:**
    *   **Central Nervous System (CNS):** Brain & Spinal Cord. Processing & control centre.
    *   **Peripheral Nervous System (PNS):** Nerves arising from CNS (Cranial nerves - 12 pairs, Spinal nerves - 31 pairs). Connects CNS to body parts. Nerve fibres: Afferent (sensory, towards CNS), Efferent (motor, away from CNS). PNS Divisions:
        *   *Somatic Nervous System (SNS):* Relays impulses from CNS to skeletal muscles (voluntary).
        *   *Autonomic Nervous System (ANS):* Transmits impulses from CNS to involuntary organs/smooth muscles. Further divided into:
            *   *Sympathetic NS:* 'Fight or flight' responses (↑HR, ↑BP, pupil dilation).
            *   *Parasympathetic NS:* 'Rest and digest' responses (↓HR, digestion stimulation). Often antagonistic effects.
*   **Brain (Encephalon):** Protected by Cranium (skull) & Meninges (Dura mater, Arachnoid mater, Pia mater). Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF) in subarachnoid space & ventricles (cushioning, nourishment). Major Parts:
    *   *Forebrain (Prosencephalon):* Cerebrum, Thalamus, Hypothalamus.
        *   *Cerebrum:* Largest part. Divided into 2 Cerebral Hemispheres connected by Corpus Callosum. Outer Cerebral Cortex ('grey matter' - cell bodies, highly folded gyri/sulci) controls sensory perception, voluntary motor actions, memory, intelligence, language. Inner 'white matter' (myelinated axons). Lobes (Frontal, Parietal, Temporal, Occipital). Association areas (complex functions like memory, communication).
        *   *Thalamus:* Relay station for sensory & motor signals to cortex.
        *   *Hypothalamus:* Below thalamus. Controls body temperature, hunger, thirst, autonomic functions, behaviour, emotions. Produces hormones regulating pituitary gland. Part of Limbic System (inner cerebrum parts + hypothalamus - emotional brain, motivation).
    *   *Midbrain (Mesencephalon):* Between thalamus & pons. Cerebral aqueduct passes through. Corpora Quadrigemina (4 lobes - reflex centres for vision/hearing).
    *   *Hindbrain (Rhombencephalon):* Pons, Cerebellum, Medulla Oblongata.
        *   *Pons:* Connects brain parts, role in respiration regulation.
        *   *Cerebellum ('Little brain'):* Coordinates voluntary muscle movements, posture, balance, equilibrium.
        *   *Medulla Oblongata:* Connects to spinal cord. Controls vital involuntary functions (respiration, heart rate, BP, vomiting, swallowing).
    *   *Brain Stem:* Midbrain + Pons + Medulla. Connects forebrain to spinal cord.
*   **Spinal Cord:** Cylindrical structure within vertebral canal. Covered by meninges. Central canal (CSF). Inner H-shaped Grey Matter (cell bodies, interneurons), Outer White Matter (myelinated tracts - ascending/sensory, descending/motor). Conducts impulses, centre for Spinal Reflexes.
*   **Reflex Action & Reflex Arc:** Rapid, involuntary, stereotyped response to stimulus. Pathway (Reflex Arc): Receptor → Afferent Pathway (sensory neuron) → Integration Centre (CNS - spinal cord/brain stem) → Efferent Pathway (motor neuron) → Effector (muscle/gland). E.g., Knee-jerk reflex (monosynaptic).
*   **Sensory Reception & Processing:**
    *   *Eye (Vision):* Structure: Layers (Sclera-outer fibrous/Cornea-transparent front, Choroid-middle vascular/Ciliary body-lens control/Iris-controls pupil, Retina-inner neural). Lens (focusing, accommodation). Aqueous humor (anterior chamber), Vitreous humor (posterior cavity). Retina: Photoreceptors (Rods - scotopic/dim light vision, Rhodopsin pigment; Cones - photopic/bright light & colour vision, 3 types of photopsins for Red/Green/Blue). Macula lutea & Fovea (central pit, max visual acuity, cones only). Blind Spot (optic nerve exits, no photoreceptors). Pathway of Light: Cornea → Aqueous humor → Pupil → Lens → Vitreous humor → Retina. Mechanism of Vision: Light → induces dissociation of retinal from opsin → changes membrane permeability → potential difference generated in photoreceptors → signal to Bipolar cells → Ganglion cells → Action potential transmitted via Optic Nerve → Visual Cortex (occipital lobe).
    *   *Ear (Hearing & Balance):* Parts:
        *   *External Ear:* Pinna (collects sound), External Auditory Meatus/Canal (conducts sound), Tympanic Membrane (Eardrum - vibrates).
        *   *Middle Ear:* Air-filled cavity. Ear Ossicles (Malleus, Incus, Stapes - amplify vibrations). Eustachian Tube (connects to pharynx, equalises pressure). Oval window & Round window (membranes to inner ear).
        *   *Inner Ear (Labyrinth):* Fluid-filled (Perilymph & Endolymph). Bony Labyrinth enclosing Membranous Labyrinth. Parts: Cochlea (hearing), Vestibular Apparatus (balance).
            *   *Cochlea:* Coiled tube. 3 chambers (Scala vestibuli, Scala media/cochlear duct, Scala tympani). Organ of Corti (on basilar membrane in scala media) contains Hair Cells (auditory receptors). Mechanism of Hearing: Sound waves → Eardrum → Ossicles → Oval window → Perilymph vibration → Basilar membrane vibration → Hair cells bend against Tectorial membrane → Nerve impulse generated in auditory nerve → Auditory Cortex (temporal lobe).
            *   *Vestibular Apparatus:* Semicircular Canals (3, perpendicular; detect rotational/angular acceleration via crista ampullaris). Otolith Organs (Utricle & Saccule; detect static position/linear acceleration via macula containing otoliths).

### Chapter: Human Physiology - Chemical Coordination and Integration (Class 11)

*   **Endocrine System:** Ductless glands secreting Hormones directly into bloodstream → travel to target organs → regulate physiology. Provides slower, widespread coordination compared to nervous system. Both systems interact.
*   **Hormones:** Non-nutrient chemicals, intercellular messengers, produced in trace amounts, specific effects.
*   **Human Endocrine Glands & Hormones:**
    *   **Hypothalamus:** Neurosecretory cells. Produces Releasing Hormones (e.g., GnRH) & Inhibiting Hormones (e.g., Somatostatin) that regulate Anterior Pituitary. Synthesises ADH & Oxytocin (transported to/released from Posterior Pituitary).
    *   **Pituitary Gland ('Master Gland'):** Located in sella turcica (sphenoid bone). Connected to hypothalamus by stalk.
        *   *Anterior Pituitary (Adenohypophysis):*
            *   Growth Hormone (GH/Somatotropin): Body growth. Disorders: Dwarfism (↓), Gigantism (↑ in child), Acromegaly (↑ in adult).
            *   Prolactin (PRL): Mammary gland growth, milk production.
            *   Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH/Thyrotropin): Stimulates thyroid gland.
            *   Adrenocorticotropic Hormone (ACTH): Stimulates adrenal cortex (glucocorticoids).
            *   Gonadotropins (LH & FSH): Luteinizing Hormone (LH): Ovulation, corpus luteum formation, progesterone (female); Testosterone synthesis (male). Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH): Ovarian follicle growth, estrogen (female); Spermatogenesis (male).
        *   *Posterior Pituitary (Neurohypophysis):* Stores & releases hypothalamic hormones:
            *   Oxytocin: Uterine contraction (parturition), milk ejection.
            *   Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH/Vasopressin): Water reabsorption by kidney tubules. Disorder: Diabetes Insipidus (↓ADH).
    *   **Pineal Gland:** Dorsal forebrain. Secretes Melatonin: Regulates 24-hr (diurnal) rhythm, sleep-wake cycle, body temp, metabolism, pigmentation, menstrual cycle, defence capability.
    *   **Thyroid Gland:** Neck region, 2 lobes connected by isthmus. Follicles synthesise Thyroid Hormones (Thyroxine/T4, Triiodothyronine/T3) - require Iodine. Functions: Regulate Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR), support RBC formation, control metabolism (carbs, proteins, fats), water/electrolyte balance, growth/development. Also secretes Calcitonin (protein hormone, regulates blood Ca++, hypocalcemic - lowers blood Ca++). Disorders: Hypothyroidism (Goitre - iodine deficiency, Cretinism - child, Myxedema - adult), Hyperthyroidism (Exophthalmic goitre/Graves' disease - ↑BMR, weight loss, protruding eyes).
    *   **Parathyroid Glands:** 4 small glands on back of thyroid. Secrete Parathyroid Hormone (PTH/Parathormone - peptide hormone): Increases blood Ca++ levels (Hypercalcemic hormone). Acts on bones (stimulates resorption/demineralisation), kidneys (↑Ca++ reabsorption), intestine (↑Ca++ absorption). Regulated by blood Ca++ levels.
    *   **Thymus Gland:** Behind sternum, above heart. Secretes Thymosins (peptide hormones): Role in differentiation of T-lymphocytes (Cell-Mediated Immunity - CMI), also promote antibody production (Humoral Immunity). Large in young, degenerates with age (weakening immunity).
    *   **Adrenal Gland ('Suprarenal'):** On top of kidneys. Outer Adrenal Cortex, Inner Adrenal Medulla.
        *   *Adrenal Cortex:* Secretes Corticosteroids (steroid hormones). 3 layers produce different types:
            *   *Glucocorticoids (mainly Cortisol):* Regulate carbohydrate metabolism (gluconeogenesis, lipolysis, proteolysis), anti-inflammatory, immunosuppressive, maintain cardiovascular/kidney function, RBC production. Regulated by ACTH. Disorder: Addison's disease (↓corticoids).
            *   *Mineralocorticoids (mainly Aldosterone):* Regulate water/electrolyte balance. Acts on renal tubules → ↑Na+ & water reabsorption, ↑K+ & phosphate excretion. Regulated by RAAS.
            *   *Sex Corticoids (Androgenic steroids):* Small amounts, role in hair growth (puberty).
        *   *Adrenal Medulla:* Secretes Catecholamines - Adrenaline (Epinephrine) & Noradrenaline (Norepinephrine). 'Emergency hormones' / 'Fight or Flight' response. Effects: ↑alertness, pupil dilation, piloerection, sweating, ↑heartbeat, ↑respiration rate, glycogenolysis (↑blood glucose), lipolysis.
    *   **Pancreas:** Mixed gland. Endocrine part: Islets of Langerhans.
        *   *α-cells:* Secrete Glucagon (peptide hormone). Hyperglycemic effect (↑blood glucose). Stimulates Glycogenolysis & Gluconeogenesis in liver.
        *   *β-cells:* Secrete Insulin (peptide hormone). Hypoglycemic effect (↓blood glucose). Enhances cellular glucose uptake/utilisation, stimulates Glycogenesis in liver/muscle. Deficiency → Diabetes Mellitus (Hyperglycemia, Glycosuria, Ketosis).
    *   **Gonads:**
        *   *Testis (Male):* Leydig/Interstitial cells produce Androgens (mainly Testosterone - steroid hormone). Functions: Development/maturation/function of male accessory sex organs, secondary sexual characters (muscle growth, hair pattern, low voice), spermatogenesis, libido. Regulated by LH.
        *   *Ovary (Female):* Produces Estrogen & Progesterone (steroid hormones). Estrogen (synthesised by growing follicles): Development/function of female accessory organs, secondary sexual characters (high voice, mammary glands), follicle development, libido. Progesterone (secreted by Corpus Luteum): Supports pregnancy, acts on mammary glands (alveoli formation, milk secretion). Regulated by FSH/LH.
*   **Hormones of Heart, Kidney, GI Tract:**
    *   *Heart (Atrial wall):* Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF - peptide). ↓Blood Pressure (causes vasodilation, opposes RAAS).
    *   *Kidney (Juxtaglomerular cells):* Erythropoietin (peptide). Stimulates Erythropoiesis (RBC formation).
    *   *Gastrointestinal Tract (Endocrine cells):* Gastrin (stimulates HCl/pepsinogen secretion), Secretin (stimulates water/bicarbonate from pancreas), Cholecystokinin (CCK - stimulates pancreatic enzymes & bile release), Gastric Inhibitory Peptide (GIP - inhibits gastric secretion/motility).
*   **Mechanism of Hormone Action:** Hormones bind specific Receptors on target cells.
    *   *Membrane-Bound Receptors:* For peptide/protein hormones, catecholamines (water-soluble). Hormone binding → Response 1 (e.g., generate Second Messenger - cAMP, IP3, Ca++) → Biochemical cascades → Physiological response.
    *   *Intracellular Receptors (mostly nuclear):* For steroid hormones, thyroid hormones (lipid-soluble). Hormone enters cell → binds receptor → Hormone-Receptor Complex interacts with genome → regulates gene expression (transcription) → Physiological response.

## UNIT 6: Reproduction

### Chapter: Reproduction in Organisms (Class 12)

*   **Reproduction:** Biological process giving rise to offspring similar to parents; ensures species continuity.
*   **Lifespan:** Period from birth to natural death; varies greatly.
*   **Asexual Reproduction:** Single parent involved; offspring are genetically identical (clones). Common in single-celled organisms, simple plants/animals.
    *   *Modes:* Fission (Binary - Amoeba, Paramecium; Multiple), Budding (Yeast, Hydra), Spore Formation (Zoospores - motile, e.g., Chlamydomonas; Conidia - non-motile, e.g., Penicillium; Gemmules - sponges), Fragmentation (Spirogyra, Hydra), Vegetative Propagation (in plants).
*   **Vegetative Propagation:** Asexual reproduction via vegetative parts (root, stem, leaf). Vegetative Propagules: Runner (grass), Rhizome (ginger), Sucker (banana), Tuber (potato - 'eyes'), Offset (water hyacinth, Pistia), Bulb (onion), Leaf buds (Bryophyllum). Water Hyacinth (Eichhornia) - 'Terror of Bengal', invasive aquatic weed, rapid offset propagation.
*   **Sexual Reproduction:** Involves formation & fusion of male & female gametes → zygote. Offspring genetically different (variation). More complex, slower process.
*   **Phases in Life Cycle:** Juvenile Phase (vegetative phase in plants) → Reproductive Phase → Senescent Phase. Transition regulated by hormones/environment. Annual, Biennial, Perennial plants. Unusual flowering: Bamboo (once in lifetime), Strobilanthes kunthiana/Neelakurinji (once in 12 years).
*   **Events in Sexual Reproduction:** Sequential events:
    *   1.  **Pre-fertilisation Events:**
        *   *Gametogenesis:* Formation of gametes (haploid). Isogametes (similar, e.g., Cladophora) vs Heterogametes (morphologically distinct - Male: antherozoid/sperm; Female: egg/ovum, e.g., Fucus, humans). Sexuality: Homothallic/Monoecious (bisexual condition) vs Heterothallic/Dioecious (unisexual condition). Flowers: Bisexual vs Unisexual (Staminate/Pistillate). Plants: Monoecious (maize, coconut) vs Dioecious (papaya, date palm). Cell division during gamete formation: Meiosis (if parent diploid), Mitosis (if parent haploid). Gamete mother cells (meiocytes) are diploid.
        *   *Gamete Transfer:* Bringing gametes together. Male gamete usually motile, female stationary (except some fungi/algae). Needs medium (water in algae, bryophytes, pteridophytes). Pollination in seed plants (transfer of pollen). Dioecious animals require mechanism for transfer (copulation).
    *   2.  **Fertilisation (Syngamy):** Fusion of gametes → diploid Zygote. Most vital event. Parthenogenesis (female gamete develops without fertilisation, e.g., rotifers, honeybees, some lizards, turkey). Fertilisation site: External (outside body, usually water - bony fish, frogs; large number of gametes produced) vs Internal (inside body - fungi, higher animals, most plants; fewer eggs, more protection).
    *   3.  **Post-fertilisation Events:** Events after zygote formation.
        *   *The Zygote:* First cell of new generation (diploid). Link between generations. Development depends on life cycle/environment. May develop immediately or undergo resting period.
        *   *Embryogenesis:* Development of embryo from zygote. Involves Cell Division (mitosis, increases cell number) and Cell Differentiation (cells specialise into tissues/organs). Development site: Oviparous animals (lay fertilised/unfertilised eggs, development outside, calcareous shell protection - reptiles, birds) vs Viviparous animals (zygote develops inside female body, young ones born - mammals). Plants: Zygote develops within ovule. Ovule → Seed, Ovary → Fruit (pericarp - fruit wall, protective). Seed dispersal.

### Chapter: Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants (Class 12)

*   **Flower:** Fascinating organ of angiosperms; site of sexual reproduction. Modified shoot.
*   **Pre-fertilisation Structures & Events:**
    *   **Stamen (Microsporophyll):** Male reproductive part. Filament (stalk) + Anther (terminal, usually bilobed/dithecous).
    *   *Structure of Anther:* Bilobed, each lobe has 2 thecae (dithecous). 4 Microsporangia located at corners, develop into Pollen Sacs. Microsporangium wall layers (Epidermis, Endothecium, Middle layers, Tapetum - nourishes developing pollen). Sporogenous tissue (centre) → Microspore Mother Cells (MMC).
    *   *Microsporogenesis:* MMC (diploid) undergoes meiosis → Microspore tetrad (haploid) → 4 Microspores (haploid).
    *   *Pollen Grain (Male Gametophyte):* Represents male gametophyte. Spherical. Two-layered wall: Exine (outer, hard, sporopollenin - resistant organic material, patterned, has germ pores) and Intine (inner, thin, cellulose + pectin). Cytoplasm contains Vegetative Cell (large, abundant food reserve, irregular nucleus) and Generative Cell (small, floats in vegetative cell cytoplasm, spindle-shaped). Pollen shed at 2-celled stage (vegetative + generative) in ~60% angiosperms; generative cell divides mitotically → 2 male gametes (3-celled stage) in others, occurs either before shedding or in pollen tube. Pollen viability varies. Economic importance (allergies, food supplements).
    *   **Pistil/Carpel (Megasporophyll):** Female reproductive part. Stigma (receptive surface for pollen), Style (elongated tube), Ovary (basal bulged part containing ovules). Gynoecium may be Monocarpellary or Multicarpellary (Syncarpous - fused, e.g., Papaver; Apocarpous - free, e.g., Michelia).
    *   *Ovule (Megasporangium):* Arises from Placenta in ovary. Attached by Funicle. Hilum (junction). Body enclosed by 1 or 2 Integuments (protective layers) leaving Micropyle opening. Chalaza (basal part, opposite micropyle). Nucellus (mass of cells within integuments, food reserve). Embryo Sac located within nucellus.
    *   *Megasporogenesis:* Megaspore Mother Cell (MMC, diploid) in nucellus undergoes meiosis → 4 Megaspores (haploid, usually linear tetrad). Usually 3 degenerate, 1 functional (towards chalaza).
    *   *Female Gametophyte (Embryo Sac):* Functional megaspore nucleus divides mitotically (3 times) → 8 nuclei arranged into 7-celled Embryo Sac (Monosporic development). Structure: Egg apparatus (at micropylar end: 1 Egg Cell + 2 Synergids with filiform apparatus guiding pollen tube), 3 Antipodal cells (at chalazal end), 1 large Central Cell (with 2 Polar Nuclei).
*   **Pollination:** Transfer of pollen from anther to stigma.
    *   *Kinds:* Autogamy (self-pollination within same flower; requires synchrony, closeness; e.g., in Chasmogamous-open flowers, or guaranteed in Cleistogamous-closed flowers - Viola, Oxalis). Geitonogamy (transfer to another flower on same plant; functionally cross-, genetically self-). Xenogamy (transfer to different plant; true cross-pollination).
    *   *Agents:* Abiotic (Wind/Anemophily - light/non-sticky pollen, feathery stigma, single ovule, e.g., grasses; Water/Hydrophily - rare, pollen protected by mucilage, e.g., Vallisneria, Hydrilla, Zostera). Biotic (Animals - majority; Insects/Entomophily - bees dominant, colourful/fragrant flowers, nectar; Birds, Bats, etc.). Floral rewards (nectar, pollen, safe place for eggs).
*   **Outbreeding Devices:** Promote cross-pollination. Unisexuality, Dichogamy (anther/stigma mature at different times), Self-incompatibility (genetic mechanism), Herkogamy (positional barrier).
*   **Pollen-Pistil Interaction:** Events from pollen deposition to pollen tube entry into ovule. Recognition of compatible pollen (chemical dialogue) → Pollen germination → Pollen tube growth through style tissues → Enters ovule (via micropyle/chalaza/integuments) → Enters embryo sac (usually via a synergid, guided by filiform apparatus). Incompatible pollen rejected. Can be manipulated (hybridisation).
*   **Artificial Hybridisation:** Crossing different varieties/species. Emasculation (removal of anthers from bisexual flower bud) + Bagging (prevent unwanted pollination) → Pollination with desired pollen → Rebagging → Fruit development.
*   **Double Fertilisation:** Unique to Angiosperms. Pollen tube releases 2 male gametes into synergid cytoplasm.
    *   *Syngamy:* 1st Male gamete (n) + Egg cell (n) → Zygote (2n).
    *   *Triple Fusion:* 2nd Male gamete (n) + Central cell (polar nuclei, n+n) → Primary Endosperm Nucleus (PEN, 3n).
*   **Post-fertilisation Structures & Events:** Sum of events after double fertilisation.
    *   *Endosperm Development:* PEN divides → Endosperm tissue (triploid, food storage). Precedes embryo development. Types: Free-nuclear (coconut water), Cellular (coconut kernel), Helobial. May be consumed by embryo (non-albuminous/exalbuminous seed - pea, bean) or persist (albuminous seed - wheat, maize, castor).
    *   *Embryo Development (Embryogeny):* Zygote develops at micropylar end. Zygote → Proembryo → Globular → Heart-shaped → Mature Embryo. Dicot Embryo: Embryonal axis + 2 Cotyledons. Axis parts: Epicotyl (above cotyledons, terminates in Plumule/shoot tip), Hypocotyl (below cotyledons, terminates in Radicle/root tip covered by root cap). Monocot Embryo: 1 large Cotyledon (Scutellum), Embryonal axis with Plumule covered by Coleoptile, Radicle covered by Coleorhiza.
    *   *Seed:* Final product of sexual reproduction; fertilised ovule. Seed coat (from integuments), Cotyledon(s), Embryonal axis. May have residual endosperm or perisperm (persistent nucellus - black pepper, beet). Dormancy. Advantages (dispersal, protection, genetic recombination). Viability varies.
    *   *Fruit:* Mature/ripened ovary. Ovary wall → Pericarp (fruit wall). Fleshy (guava, mango) or Dry (groundnut, mustard). True fruit (from ovary only) vs False fruit (thalamus also contributes - apple, strawberry). Parthenocarpic fruits (develop without fertilisation, seedless - banana).
*   **Apomixis & Polyembryony:**
    *   *Apomixis:* Seed production without fertilisation (asexual reproduction mimicking sexual). E.g., Asteraceae, grasses. Clonal progeny. Importance in hybrid seed industry.
    *   *Polyembryony:* Occurrence of more than one embryo in a seed (e.g., Citrus, Mango).

### Chapter: Human Reproduction (Class 12)

*   **Human Reproduction:** Sexual, Viviparous. Events: Gametogenesis (sperm/ovum formation), Insemination (sperm transfer to female tract), Fertilisation (gamete fusion → zygote), Implantation (blastocyst attachment to uterus), Gestation (embryonic development), Parturition (childbirth). Events occur after puberty.
*   **Male Reproductive System:** Located in pelvis region. Includes: Testes (pair), Accessory Ducts, Accessory Glands, External Genitalia.
    *   *Testes:* Primary sex organs. Situated outside abdomen in Scrotum (maintains lower temp, 2-2.5°C less than body, for spermatogenesis). Oval shape. Covered by tunica albuginea. Each testis has ~250 Testicular Lobules, each containing 1-3 Seminiferous Tubules (site of sperm production). Tubules lined by Male germ cells (spermatogonia) & Sertoli cells (provide nourishment). Interstitial space contains Leydig cells/Interstitial cells (synthesise/secrete androgens - Testosterone).
    *   *Accessory Ducts:* Seminiferous tubules → Rete testis → Vasa efferentia → Epididymis (long coiled tube, sperm storage/maturation) → Vas deferens (ascends to abdomen, loops over bladder) → receives duct from Seminal vesicle → forms Ejaculatory duct → passes through Prostate → opens into Urethra (originates from bladder, extends through penis).
    *   *Accessory Glands:* Paired Seminal Vesicles, single Prostate gland, paired Bulbo-urethral glands (Cowper's glands). Secretions constitute Seminal Plasma (rich in fructose, calcium, enzymes). Semen = Seminal plasma + Sperm. Functions: transport, nutrition, activation of sperm. Bulbo-urethral secretion lubricates penis.
    *   *External Genitalia:* Penis (copulatory organ, erectile tissue for insemination). Glans penis (enlarged end) covered by Foreskin (prepuce).
*   **Female Reproductive System:** Located in pelvis region. Includes: Ovaries (pair), Oviducts (pair), Uterus, Cervix, Vagina, External Genitalia, Mammary Glands (pair).
    *   *Ovaries:* Primary sex organs. Almond-shaped. Produce Ovum (female gamete) & Steroid hormones (Estrogen, Progesterone). Connected to pelvic wall/uterus by ligaments. Covered by epithelium enclosing ovarian stroma (cortex & medulla). Cortex contains Ovarian Follicles at different stages.
    *   *Oviducts (Fallopian Tubes):* ~10-12 cm long. Extend from ovary periphery to uterus. Parts: Infundibulum (funnel-shaped near ovary, edges have Fimbriae - collect ovum), Ampulla (wider part, site of fertilisation), Isthmus (narrow part joining uterus).
    *   *Uterus (Womb):* Single, inverted pear shape. Supported by ligaments. Site of implantation & fetal development. Wall layers: Perimetrium (outer thin membrane), Myometrium (middle thick smooth muscle - contractions during delivery), Endometrium (inner glandular layer, undergoes cyclic changes during menstrual cycle, implantation site).
    *   *Cervix:* Narrow lower part of uterus opening into vagina. Cervical canal + Vagina = Birth canal.
    *   *Vagina:* Muscular tube receiving semen during copulation, serves as birth canal.
    *   *External Genitalia (Vulva):* Mons pubis (fatty tissue cushion, skin/pubic hair), Labia majora (fleshy folds surrounding vaginal opening), Labia minora (paired inner folds), Hymen (membrane partially covering vaginal opening), Clitoris (tiny finger-like structure at upper junction of labia minora).
    *   *Mammary Glands:* Paired structures (breasts) containing glandular tissue & fat. Glandular tissue → 15-20 Mammary Lobes → Clusters of cells (Alveoli - secrete milk) → Mammary Tubules → Mammary Ducts → Mammary Ampulla → Lactiferous Duct → Nipple (milk sucked out). Functional only post-pregnancy.
*   **Gametogenesis:**
    *   *Spermatogenesis:* Sperm formation in testes (seminiferous tubules). Starts at puberty. Spermatogonia (2n) -(Mitosis)→ increase number. Some Spermatogonia -(Growth)→ Primary Spermatocytes (2n) -(Meiosis I)→ 2 Secondary Spermatocytes (n) -(Meiosis II)→ 4 Spermatids (n). Spermatids transform into Spermatozoa (Sperm) via Spermiogenesis. Spermiation (release of sperm from Sertoli cells). Hormonal control: GnRH → LH (acts on Leydig cells → Androgens → stimulate spermatogenesis) & FSH (acts on Sertoli cells → factors for spermiogenesis).
    *   *Sperm Structure:* Head (contains haploid nucleus, capped by Acrosome - enzyme-filled, helps fertilisation), Neck, Middle Piece (numerous mitochondria - energy for motility), Tail (flagellum - motility). ~200-300 million sperm per ejaculate.
    *   *Oogenesis:* Ovum formation in ovary. Initiated during fetal development. Oogonia (2n) -(Mitosis)→ Primary Oocytes (2n) formed by birth, arrested in Prophase I. At puberty: Primary Oocyte completes Meiosis I → Large Secondary Oocyte (n) + tiny First Polar Body (n). Secondary Oocyte arrested in Metaphase II. Ovulation releases Secondary Oocyte. Meiosis II completed only upon sperm entry → Ovum (Ootid, n) + Second Polar Body (n). Follicle development: Primary follicle → Secondary → Tertiary (fluid-filled antrum) → Mature Graafian follicle (contains secondary oocyte). After ovulation, ruptured follicle → Corpus Luteum.
*   **Menstrual Cycle:** Cyclic changes in reproductive tract (ovary/uterus) of female primates (~28/29 days). Starts at Puberty (Menarche), ceases around 50 yrs (Menopause). Cycle Phases:
    *   1.  *Menstrual Phase (Day 1-5):* Endometrial lining breaks down, blood vessels rupture → bleeding (menstruation). Occurs if ovum not fertilised. Due to ↓Progesterone/Estrogen (Corpus luteum degenerated).
    *   2.  *Follicular Phase (Proliferative Phase, Day 5-14):* Primary follicles grow → Mature Graafian follicle. Endometrium regenerates. Driven by Gonadotropins (FSH, LH) increase. Growing follicles secrete Estrogen → stimulates endometrial proliferation.
    *   3.  *Ovulatory Phase (~Day 14):* Rapid LH secretion (LH Surge) induces Graafian follicle rupture → Ovulation (release of secondary oocyte). FSH also peaks.
    *   4.  *Luteal Phase (Secretory Phase, Day 14-28):* Remaining follicle parts → Corpus Luteum (secretes large amounts of Progesterone - essential for maintaining endometrium for implantation). Endometrium further thickens. If fertilisation occurs, Corpus luteum maintained (by hCG). If not, Corpus luteum degenerates → ↓Progesterone → Menstruation.
*   **Fertilisation & Implantation:**
    *   *Fertilisation:* Fusion of sperm & ovum in Ampulla of oviduct. Sperm reach ampulla, ovum released → simultaneous arrival needed. Sperm undergoes Capacitation → Acrosome reaction (enzymes release) → penetrates Corona Radiata & Zona Pellucida. Sperm entry induces completion of Meiosis II of secondary oocyte → Ovum + 2nd polar body. Fusion of sperm & ovum nuclei → diploid Zygote. Cortical reaction prevents polyspermy. Sex determined by sperm (X or Y).
    *   *Cleavage:* Mitotic divisions of zygote as it moves through isthmus → 2, 4, 8, 16 daughter cells (Blastomeres). Embryo with 8-16 blastomeres = Morula.
    *   *Blastocyst Formation:* Morula continues dividing → Blastocyst (hollow ball). Outer layer = Trophoblast (attaches to endometrium), Inner group of cells = Inner Cell Mass (gives rise to embryo).
    *   *Implantation:* Blastocyst embeds in Endometrium of uterus (~Day 6-7 after fertilisation). Leads to Pregnancy.
*   **Pregnancy & Embryonic Development:**
    *   After implantation, finger-like Chorionic Villi appear on trophoblast, interdigitate with uterine tissue → form Placenta (structural/functional unit between fetus & mother).
    *   *Placenta:* Facilitates nutrient/O2 supply to fetus, CO2/waste removal from fetus. Connected to fetus by Umbilical Cord. Acts as Endocrine gland (secretes hCG, hPL, Estrogens, Progestogens; Relaxin later). hCG maintains corpus luteum initially.
    *   *Embryonic Development:* Inner cell mass differentiates into 3 Germ Layers (Ectoderm, Mesoderm, Endoderm) → give rise to all tissues/organs (Organogenesis). Stem cells in inner cell mass. Major features develop month-wise (Heart sounds - 1st month; Limbs/digits - 2nd month; Major organs, external genitalia - 1st trimester/12 weeks; Fetal movements, head hair - 5th month; Body hair, eyelids separate, eyelashes - 2nd trimester/24 weeks; Fully developed, ready for delivery - 9 months).
*   **Parturition & Lactation:**
    *   *Parturition (Childbirth):* Process of delivery. Vigorous uterine contractions at end of pregnancy (Gestation period ~9 months). Complex neuroendocrine mechanism. Signals from fully developed fetus & placenta → mild contractions (Fetal Ejection Reflex) → triggers Oxytocin release (posterior pituitary) → stronger contractions → further oxytocin release (positive feedback) → expulsion of baby. Followed by expulsion of placenta.
    *   *Lactation:* Mammary glands produce milk towards end of pregnancy (stimulated by Prolactin). Colostrum (initial milk) rich in antibodies (IgA) providing passive immunity to newborn. Breastfeeding recommended.

### Chapter: Reproductive Health (Class 12)

*   **Reproductive Health:** Total well-being (physical, emotional, behavioural, social) in all aspects of reproduction (WHO). India initiated family planning (1951). Reproductive & Child Health care (RCH) programs cover wider aspects.
*   **Problems & Strategies:** Population explosion (causes: ↓death rate, ↑lifespan, ↓MMR/IMR). Need for awareness (sex education), facilities, contraception, MTP control, STI prevention, infertility management. Raising marriageable age.
*   **Population Stabilisation & Birth Control:** Need to control population growth. Contraceptive Methods: Prevent unwanted pregnancies. Ideal contraceptive: user-friendly, effective, reversible, no/least side effects, not interfere with sexual drive. Methods:
    *   *Natural/Traditional:* Avoid chances of sperm-ovum meeting. Periodic Abstinence (avoid coitus day 10-17 of cycle), Withdrawal/Coitus Interruptus, Lactational Amenorrhea (absence of menstruation post-partum, effective up to 6 months if intense lactation, no menstruation). High failure rates.
    *   *Barrier Methods:* Prevent physical meeting. Condoms (male/female, disposable, latex/rubber; also prevent STIs), Diaphragms, Cervical caps, Vaults (female, reusable, cover cervix, used with spermicides).
    *   *Intra Uterine Devices (IUDs):* Inserted into uterus by experts. Non-medicated (Lippes Loop - increase phagocytosis of sperm), Copper releasing (CuT, Cu7, Multiload 375 - suppress sperm motility/fertilising capacity), Hormone releasing (Progestasert, LNG-20 - make uterus unsuitable, thicken cervical mucus). Most widely accepted in India. Increase sperm phagocytosis, alter uterine environment.
    *   *Oral Contraceptives ('Pills'):* Progestogen alone or Progestogen-Estrogen combination. Taken daily for 21 days (starting within first 5 days of cycle), gap of 7 days. Inhibit ovulation & implantation, alter cervical mucus. Very effective, fewer side effects. 'Saheli' (non-steroidal, once-a-week pill, developed by CDRI Lucknow).
    *   *Injectables/Implants:* Progestogen alone or combination. Under skin. Longer effective period than pills. Similar action.
    *   *Emergency Contraceptives:* Progestogens or Progestogen-Estrogen combo, or IUDs. Used within 72 hours of unprotected coitus/rape/casual sex. Prevents pregnancy.
    *   *Surgical Methods (Sterilisation):* Terminal method, highly effective, poor reversibility. Vasectomy (male: small part of vas deferens removed/tied), Tubectomy (female: small part of fallopian tube removed/tied). Blocks gamete transport.
*   **Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP):** Intentional/voluntary termination before full term (Induced Abortion). Legalised in India (MTP Act, 1971) with restrictions (avoid misuse like female foeticide). Done to get rid of unwanted pregnancies (contraceptive failure, rape) or if continuation harmful to mother/fetus. Safer during first trimester (up to 12 weeks). Requires qualified medical personnel.
*   **Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs):** Infections transmitted via sexual contact (Venereal Diseases/VD or Reproductive Tract Infections/RTI). Common examples: Gonorrhoea, Syphilis, Genital Herpes, Chlamydiasis, Genital Warts, Trichomoniasis, Hepatitis B, HIV/AIDS. Hepatitis B & HIV also spread via needles, blood transfusion, mother to fetus. Except Hep-B, Herpes, AIDS, others curable if detected early. Early symptoms often minor/absent (itching, discharge, pain, swelling). Can lead to complications (PID, abortion, stillbirth, ectopic pregnancy, infertility, cancer). Prevention: Avoid sex with unknown/multiple partners, use condoms, consult doctor if in doubt. Early detection, partner treatment crucial.
*   **Infertility:** Inability to produce children despite unprotected cohabitation. Reasons: physical, congenital, diseases, drugs, immunological, psychological. Correctable in many cases.
*   **Assisted Reproductive Technologies (ART):** Help infertile couples.
    *   *In Vitro Fertilisation (IVF) - 'Test Tube Baby':* Ova (wife/donor) + Sperm (husband/donor) fertilised outside body in lab ('in vitro') → Zygote/Early embryo formed. Followed by Embryo Transfer (ET).
    *   *ZIFT (Zygote Intra Fallopian Transfer):* Zygote or early embryo (up to 8 blastomeres) transferred into Fallopian tube.
    *   *IUT (Intra Uterine Transfer):* Embryo with >8 blastomeres transferred into Uterus.
    *   *GIFT (Gamete Intra Fallopian Transfer):* Transfer of Ovum (donor) into Fallopian tube of female who cannot produce ovum but provides suitable environment.
    *   *ICSI (Intra Cytoplasmic Sperm Injection):* Sperm directly injected into ovum cytoplasm (in lab).
    *   *AI (Artificial Insemination):* Semen (husband/donor) artificially introduced into vagina or uterus (IUI - Intra Uterine Insemination). Used in male infertility (low sperm count/motility, inability to inseminate).
*   **Options:** ART requires high precision, expensive facilities. Emotional, religious, social factors involved. Adoption is another option.

## UNIT 7: Genetics and Evolution

### Chapter: Principles of Inheritance and Variation (Class 12)

*   **Genetics:** Study of Heredity (transmission of characters parent → offspring) and Variation (differences among individuals). Inheritance is the basis.
*   **Mendel's Laws of Inheritance:** Gregor Mendel ('Father of Genetics') conducted hybridisation experiments on Garden Pea (*Pisum sativum*) for 7 years (mid-19th century). Used statistical analysis. Selected 14 true-breeding pea varieties (7 pairs of contrasting traits: Stem height, Flower colour/position, Pod shape/colour, Seed shape/colour).
*   **Inheritance of One Gene (Monohybrid Cross):** Cross involving one pair of contrasting traits (e.g., Tall x Dwarf). F1 generation resembled one parent (Tall). F2 generation showed both traits in ratio 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf. Mendel proposed 'Factors' (now Genes) controlling traits, occur in pairs. Alleles = alternative forms of a gene (T, t). Homozygous (TT/tt) vs Heterozygous (Tt). Phenotype (observable) vs Genotype (genetic makeup). Dominant allele (expresses in F1/heterozygote) vs Recessive allele (masked). Punnett Square (R.C. Punnett) predicts progeny genotypes/phenotypes. Test Cross (cross F1/unknown dominant genotype with homozygous recessive parent) to determine genotype of dominant phenotype organism.
    *   *Law of Dominance:* Characters controlled by factors; factors in pairs; one factor dominates other in dissimilar pair. (Explains F1 expression, 3:1 F2 ratio).
    *   *Law of Segregation:* Alleles do not blend; they segregate during gamete formation; gamete receives only one of the two factors. (Explains F2 reappearance of recessive trait). Universal law.
*   **Deviations from Mendelism (Gene Interaction):**
    *   *Incomplete Dominance:* F1 phenotype is intermediate between parents (e.g., Flower colour in Snapdragon/*Antirrhinum* or *Mirabilis jalapa* - Red x White → Pink F1; F2 ratio 1 Red : 2 Pink : 1 White - Phenotypic & Genotypic ratios same 1:2:1). Allele doesn't completely dominate.
    *   *Co-dominance:* Both alleles express themselves fully & equally in heterozygote (e.g., ABO blood groups in humans - IAIB genotype shows both A & B antigens).
    *   *Multiple Alleles:* More than two alleles exist for a gene in a population (but individual has only two). E.g., ABO blood group gene (I) has 3 alleles: IA, IB, i. (IA, IB dominant over i; IA, IB co-dominant). Results in 4 phenotypes (A, B, AB, O) and 6 genotypes. Example of dominance, co-dominance, multiple alleles.
*   **Inheritance of Two Genes (Dihybrid Cross):** Cross involving two pairs of contrasting traits (e.g., Round Yellow seed x Wrinkled Green seed). F1 all Round Yellow. F2 generation showed 4 phenotypes in ratio 9 (R_Y_) : 3 (R_ww) : 3 (rrY_) : 1 (rryy).
    *   *Law of Independent Assortment:* When two pairs of traits are combined, segregation of one pair of characters is independent of the other pair. (Based on dihybrid cross F2 ratio). Applicable for genes on different chromosomes or far apart on same chromosome.
*   **Pleiotropy:** Single gene affects multiple phenotypic traits (e.g., Phenylketonuria - mutation affects mental retardation, skin/hair pigment; Starch synthesis gene in pea affects seed shape & size).
*   **Polygenic Inheritance:** Trait controlled by multiple genes (polygenes); effect is additive. Shows continuous variation, influenced by environment (quantitative inheritance). E.g., Human skin colour, height.
*   **Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance (Sutton & Boveri, 1902):** Parallel behaviour of Genes (Mendel's factors) & Chromosomes during meiosis & fertilisation. Chromosomes are vehicles of heredity. Pairing/separation of chromosomes leads to segregation of factors they carry. Independent assortment due to independent segregation of chromosome pairs. Experimental verification by T.H. Morgan on *Drosophila melanogaster* (fruit fly).
*   **Linkage & Recombination:** Morgan's work showed deviation from independent assortment for genes on same chromosome. Physical association of genes on a chromosome = Linkage. Linked genes tend to be inherited together. Recombination = generation of non-parental gene combinations due to Crossing Over between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes during meiosis (Pachytene). Frequency of recombination between gene pairs is measure of distance between them (used by Alfred Sturtevant to map gene positions on chromosome - genetic maps). Tightly linked genes show low recombination, loosely linked show high recombination.
*   **Sex Determination:** Mechanism establishing sex.
    *   *Chromosomal Basis:* Sex chromosomes (X, Y, Z, W) vs Autosomes.
        *   *XX-XY type:* Humans, Drosophila. Female XX (homogametic), Male XY (heterogametic). Male determines sex of offspring.
        *   *XX-XO type:* Grasshopper. Female XX, Male XO (one X missing). Male produces sperm with X or no X chromosome.
        *   *ZZ-ZW type:* Birds. Female ZW (heterogametic), Male ZZ (homogametic). Female determines sex.
    *   *Sex Determination in Humans:* 22 pairs autosomes + XX (female) or XY (male). Y chromosome carries gene (SRY) for maleness.
    *   *Sex Determination in Honey Bee (Haplodiploidy):* Female (Queen/Worker) develops from fertilised egg (diploid, 32 chr.). Male (Drone) develops from unfertilised egg via parthenogenesis (haploid, 16 chr.). Drones produce sperm by mitosis.
*   **Mutation:** Sudden heritable change in genetic material (DNA sequence). Source of new alleles/variation. Can be Point Mutation (change in single base pair, e.g., Sickle-cell anaemia - GAG to GUG in beta-globin gene) or Frameshift Mutation (insertion/deletion of bases, changes reading frame). Mutagens (agents causing mutation - radiation, chemicals).
*   **Genetic Disorders:**
    *   **Pedigree Analysis:** Study of trait inheritance in human families using standard symbols in a pedigree chart. Helps trace inheritance pattern (autosomal/sex-linked, dominant/recessive), predict risk.
    *   **Mendelian Disorders:** Caused by alteration/mutation in single gene. Follow Mendelian inheritance patterns.
        *   *Autosomal Recessive:* Haemophilia (X-linked), Colour Blindness (X-linked), Sickle-cell Anaemia (point mutation, Glu→Val at 6th pos of β-globin; heterozygous carrier resistant to malaria), Phenylketonuria (PKU - enzyme deficiency → phenylalanine accumulation → mental retardation), Thalassemia (defect in globin chain synthesis, α/β; quantitative defect), Cystic Fibrosis.
        *   *Autosomal Dominant:* Myotonic dystrophy.
        *   *Sex-linked Recessive:* Haemophilia (defect in clotting factor), Colour Blindness (red-green). More common in males.
    *   **Chromosomal Disorders:** Caused by absence/excess/abnormal arrangement of one or more chromosomes. Due to errors in segregation (Nondisjunction) during meiosis.
        *   *Aneuploidy:* Gain/loss of chromosome(s). Trisomy (2n+1), Monosomy (2n-1).
            *   *Down's Syndrome:* Trisomy of chromosome 21. Symptoms: Short stature, small round head, furrowed tongue, partially open mouth, broad palm (palmar crease), physical/psychomotor/mental retardation.
            *   *Klinefelter's Syndrome:* Trisomy of sex chromosome in males (XXY). Overall masculine development but feminine characteristics (gynaecomastia), sterile.
            *   *Turner's Syndrome:* Monosomy of sex chromosome in females (XO). Sterile females, rudimentary ovaries, lack of secondary sexual characters.

### Chapter: Molecular Basis of Inheritance (Class 12)

*   **The Genetic Material:** DNA established as predominant genetic material. RNA in some viruses. Criteria for genetic material: Replication, Stability (chemical/structural), Mutation (slow change for evolution), Expression (Mendelian characters via RNA/protein). DNA more stable than RNA (2'-OH group in RNA makes it labile/catalytic).
*   **Search for Genetic Material:**
    *   *Griffith's Experiment (1928):* Transformation in *Streptococcus pneumoniae*. Heat-killed S-strain transformed live R-strain into virulent S-strain. 'Transforming Principle'.
    *   *Avery, MacLeod, McCarty (1944):* Biochemical characterisation. Showed DNA (not protein/RNA) was the transforming principle (using proteases, RNases, DNases).
    *   *Hershey & Chase Experiment (1952):* Bacteriophages (T2) labelled with radioactive 32P (in DNA) or 35S (in protein). Infection of *E. coli*. Found 32P entered bacteria, 35S remained outside. Conclusively proved DNA is genetic material.
*   **RNA World:** RNA was likely first genetic material & catalyst (ribozyme). DNA evolved later (more stable).
*   **DNA Structure:** Polymer of deoxyribonucleotides. Nucleotide = Deoxyribose Sugar + Nitrogenous Base (A,G,C,T) + Phosphate group. Linked by phosphodiester bonds (between 5'-phosphate and 3'-OH of sugar). Polynucleotide chain has 5' end (free phosphate) and 3' end (free OH).
    *   *Double Helix (Watson & Crick, 1953):* Based on Chargaff's rules (A=T, G=C; A+G = T+C) & X-ray diffraction (Franklin, Wilkins). Features: Two antiparallel chains (5'→3' & 3'→5'); Sugar-phosphate backbone; Bases project inwards, pair via H-bonds (A=T 2 H-bonds, G≡C 3 H-bonds); Helical turn 3.4 nm (10 bp/turn); Distance between bp 0.34 nm; Diameter ~2 nm. Stability due to H-bonds & base stacking. B-DNA is common form.
*   **DNA Packaging:** Length of human DNA (~2.2m) fits in small nucleus (~10^-6m).
    *   *Prokaryotes:* DNA (no histones) held with some proteins in Nucleoid region, large loops.
    *   *Eukaryotes:* DNA wrapped around Histone octamer (positively charged proteins H2A, H2B, H3, H4 x2) → Nucleosome (~200 bp DNA per nucleosome). H1 histone links nucleosomes. Nucleosomes condense → Chromatin ('beads-on-string') → further coiling → Chromatin fibre → Chromatid → Chromosome. Euchromatin (loosely packed, transcriptionally active) vs Heterochromatin (densely packed, inactive).
*   **DNA Replication:** Process of making identical DNA copies. Occurs in S-phase. Semi-conservative (Meselson & Stahl experiment using heavy nitrogen 15N). Requires: DNA template, DNA Polymerase (main enzyme, 5'→3' polymerisation, proofreading), Helicase (unwinds), Primase (synthesises RNA primer), DNA Ligase (joins fragments), dNTPs (substrates + energy). Origin of Replication (ori). Replication Fork (Y-shaped). Leading strand (synthesised continuously 5'→3' towards fork). Lagging strand (synthesised discontinuously as Okazaki fragments 5'→3' away from fork, joined by ligase). Bidirectional. High fidelity.
*   **Transcription:** Synthesis of RNA from DNA template strand. Catalysed by RNA Polymerase. Principle of complementarity (A-U, G-C). Occurs in nucleus (eukaryotes), cytoplasm (prokaryotes). Unit = Promoter + Structural gene + Terminator. Promoter (upstream, RNA Pol binding site). Terminator (downstream, stops transcription). Template strand (3'→5', transcribed) vs Coding strand (5'→3', same sequence as RNA except T/U). Structural gene codes for RNA.
    *   *Prokaryotes:* Single RNA Pol makes all RNAs. Initiation (σ factor recognizes promoter), Elongation, Termination (ρ factor or hairpin loop). mRNA often polycistronic. Transcription & Translation coupled.
    *   *Eukaryotes:* 3 RNA Pols (I: rRNAs; II: hnRNA/mRNA precursor; III: tRNA, 5S rRNA, snRNAs). More complex promoters (TATA box). Transcription factors needed. Post-transcriptional processing of hnRNA → mature mRNA: Splicing (removal of introns, joining of exons by spliceosome), Capping (methyl guanosine triphosphate at 5' end), Tailing (polyadenylate residues at 3' end). mRNA monocistronic. Transported to cytoplasm for translation.
*   **Genetic Code:** Relationship between nucleotide sequence (mRNA) & amino acid sequence (protein). Codon = triplet of bases. Features: Triplet, Universal (mostly), Degenerate (multiple codons for one AA, except Met/Trp), Unambiguous (one codon → one AA), Comma-less, Non-overlapping. Start codon (AUG - Met). Stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA). Wobble hypothesis (relaxation in pairing at 3rd codon base).
*   **Mutations & Genetic Code:** Point mutations (silent, missense, nonsense). Frameshift mutations (insertion/deletion).
*   **tRNA (Transfer RNA):** Adaptor molecule. Reads codon (Anticodon loop), carries specific amino acid (Amino acid acceptor end - 3' CCA). Cloverleaf structure (2D), inverted L-shape (3D). Specific tRNAs for each amino acid. Aminoacylation (charging) of tRNA by aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase.
*   **Translation (Protein Synthesis):** Polymerisation of amino acids → polypeptide, sequence dictated by mRNA. Occurs on Ribosomes (rRNA + proteins; large & small subunits; sites A, P, E). Steps:
    *   1.  *Activation of Amino Acids & Charging of tRNA.*
    *   2.  *Initiation:* Small ribosomal subunit binds mRNA; Initiator tRNA (Met) binds start codon (AUG) at P site; Large subunit joins → forms initiation complex. Requires GTP, initiation factors.
    *   3.  *Elongation:* Next charged tRNA enters A site → Peptide bond forms between AA at P site & A site (catalysed by peptidyl transferase - ribozyme activity of rRNA) → Ribosome moves (translocates) one codon along mRNA (requires GTP) → tRNA at P site moves to E site (exits), tRNA at A site moves to P site. Cycle repeats.
    *   4.  *Termination:* Stop codon reaches A site → Release Factor binds → Polypeptide released, ribosomal subunits dissociate. Requires GTP.
*   **Regulation of Gene Expression:** Control of gene activity level/timing. Can occur at transcriptional, processing, transport, translational levels. Eukaryotes: Complex regulation (chromatin remodelling, transcription factors, alternative splicing, RNAi). Prokaryotes: Simpler, often via Operons.
    *   *Lac Operon (Jacob & Monod):* In *E. coli*, regulates lactose metabolism. Inducible operon. Components: Regulator gene (i - makes repressor protein), Promoter (p), Operator (o), Structural genes (z-β-galactosidase, y-permease, a-transacetylase). Mechanism: Absence of lactose (inducer) → Repressor binds Operator → blocks RNA Pol → No transcription. Presence of lactose → Allolactose (inducer) binds Repressor → Repressor conformation change, cannot bind Operator → RNA Pol binds Promoter → Transcription of structural genes → enzymes produced. Also subject to positive control (CAP-cAMP).
*   **Human Genome Project (HGP):** International effort (1990-2003) to sequence entire human genome. Goals: Identify all genes, sequence 3 billion bp, store data, address ELSI (Ethical, Legal, Social Issues). Methodologies: Expressed Sequence Tags (ESTs - identifying genes expressed as RNA), Sequence Annotation (sequencing whole genome, assigning function). Used Sanger sequencing, BAC/YAC vectors. Bioinformatics crucial. Salient Features: ~3.16 billion bp; ~20,000-25,000 genes (fewer than expected); Avg gene ~3000 bp; <2% codes protein; Large portion is Repetitive sequences; Chromosome 1 has most genes, Y has fewest; ~1.4 million SNPs identified. Applications: Disease diagnosis/treatment, understanding human biology/evolution.
*   **DNA Fingerprinting:** Technique to identify individuals based on DNA sequence variations. Principle: Uses Satellite DNA (repetitive sequences) esp. Variable Number of Tandem Repeats (VNTRs - 'minisatellites') which show high degree of polymorphism (variation). Steps: DNA isolation → Restriction digestion → Gel electrophoresis (separate fragments by size) → Southern Blotting (transfer DNA to membrane) → Hybridisation with labelled VNTR probe → Autoradiography (detect hybridised fragments → pattern unique to individual). Developed by Alec Jeffreys. Applications: Forensics, Paternity testing, Population genetics.

### Chapter: Evolution (Class 12)

*   **Evolutionary Biology:** Study of history of life forms on Earth.
*   **Origin of Life:** Universe originated ~20 bya (Big Bang Theory). Earth formed ~4.5 bya. Life appeared ~4 bya. Early atmosphere reducing (no free O2).
    *   *Chemical Evolution (Oparin-Haldane):* First life arose from non-living organic molecules (RNA, protein) formed from inorganic constituents under primitive Earth conditions (energy from lightning, UV).
    *   *Miller-Urey Experiment (1953):* Simulated primitive conditions (H2, CH4, NH3, H2O vapour, electric sparks) → produced amino acids. Supports chemical evolution.
    *   *First Cells:* Non-cellular aggregates (coacervates/microspheres) → First cellular life (~2 bya, single-celled, anaerobic prokaryotes). Autotrophs (chemo-/photo-) evolved later, releasing O2.
*   **Theories of Evolution of Life Forms:** Special Creation (rejected). Spontaneous Generation (Abiogenesis - life from non-living; disproved by Pasteur's swan-neck flask experiment). Biogenesis (life from pre-existing life). Panspermia (life from spores from space). Organic/Chemical Evolution (widely accepted).
*   **Evidences for Evolution:**
    *   *Paleontological Evidence:* Fossils (remains/impressions in rocks). Show extinct forms, links between groups, gradual changes. Fossil dating (radiometric dating).
    *   *Comparative Anatomy & Morphology:* Homologous Structures (similar structure/origin, different function; indicate common ancestry, Divergent evolution; e.g., vertebrate forelimbs, thorn/tendril of Bougainvillea/Cucurbita). Analogous Structures (different structure/origin, similar function; indicate Convergent evolution; e.g., wings of butterfly/birds, eye of octopus/mammals, sweet potato/potato). Vestigial Organs (reduced/non-functional remnants of ancestral organs). Atavism (reappearance of ancestral trait). Embryological Evidence (similarities in early embryos; Haeckel's Biogenetic Law 'Ontogeny recapitulates Phylogeny' - not fully accepted; von Baer's law - general features appear before specific ones).
    *   *Biogeographical Evidence:* Distribution patterns of organisms (e.g., Darwin's Finches on Galapagos - adaptive radiation; Australian Marsupials - adaptive radiation in isolation). Continental drift.
    *   *Molecular Evidence:* Similarities in DNA, RNA, proteins, metabolic pathways indicate common ancestry. Universal genetic code.
    *   *Artificial Selection:* Human-driven selection for desired traits in domestic plants/animals.
    *   *Observed Natural Selection:* Industrial Melanism in Peppered Moth (*Biston betularia* - England; differential survival based on camouflage against polluted/unpolluted backgrounds). Herbicide/Pesticide resistance. Antibiotic resistance in microbes.
*   **Theories of Biological Evolution:**
    *   *Lamarckism:* Theory of Inheritance of Acquired Characters (Use & Disuse). Changes acquired during lifetime passed to offspring (e.g., giraffe neck). Largely disproved.
    *   *Darwinism (Theory of Natural Selection):* Proposed by Charles Darwin (based on voyage of H.M.S. Beagle) & Alfred Wallace. Key concepts: Overproduction, Variation (heritable differences within population), Struggle for Existence (intra-/interspecific, environmental), Differential Survival & Reproduction ('Survival of the Fittest' - based on reproductive fitness). Natural Selection acts on variations, leads to adaptation and evolution of new species over time. Weakness: Didn't explain source/inheritance of variation.
    *   *Mutation Theory (Hugo de Vries):* Evolution due to sudden large mutations (Saltation), not small variations. Mutations are random, directionless (vs Darwinian variations - small, directional).
    *   *Modern Synthetic Theory:* Integration of Darwinism with genetics, paleontology, systematics etc. Main factors: Genetic Variation (Mutation, Recombination, Gene flow), Natural Selection, Genetic Drift, Isolation. Population is unit of evolution.
*   **Hardy-Weinberg Principle:** In a large, random-mating population, allele & genotype frequencies remain constant generation after generation if no evolutionary influences are acting (Genetic Equilibrium). Equation: p² + 2pq + q² = 1 (genotype freq.); p + q = 1 (allele freq.). p = freq(A), q = freq(a). Deviation from equilibrium indicates evolution. Factors affecting: Gene Migration/Flow, Genetic Drift (random changes, esp. in small populations - Founder Effect, Bottleneck Effect), Mutation, Genetic Recombination, Natural Selection.
*   **Types of Natural Selection:** Stabilising (favours average phenotype, eliminates extremes), Directional (favours one extreme, shifts mean), Disruptive (favours both extremes, eliminates average).
*   **Speciation:** Formation of new species. Requires Reproductive Isolation (prevents interbreeding between populations).
*   **Brief Account of Evolution:** ~500 mya: Invertebrates formed. ~350 mya: Jawless fish evolved → Fish with stout fins (lobefins) → first Amphibians (ancestors of reptiles/mammals). Reptiles dominated (~200 mya), some land reptiles → back to water (Ichthyosaurs). Dinosaurs appeared, largest land reptiles, disappeared ~65 mya. First mammals (shrew-like) appeared, viviparous. Birds evolved from reptiles. Continental drift affected distribution. Mammals diversified after dinosaur extinction.
*   **Origin and Evolution of Man:** Order Primates. Dryopithecus (ape-like) & Ramapithecus (man-like) → Hominid line diverged.
    *   *Australopithecus:* ~2 mya, East Africa. Hunted with stones, ate fruit. ~450cc brain. First human-like hominid. Bipedalism started.
    *   *Homo habilis:* ~2 mya. ~650-800cc brain. 'Handy man' - made tools. Probably didn't eat meat.
    *   *Homo erectus:* ~1.5 mya. ~900cc brain. Walked erect. Ate meat, used fire. Migrated out of Africa (Java Man etc.).
    *   *Homo neanderthalensis (Neanderthal Man):* ~100,000 - 40,000 ya. Europe, Asia. ~1400cc brain (larger than modern humans). Used hides, buried dead, complex tools. Co-existed with H. sapiens.
    *   *Homo sapiens (Modern Man):* Arose in Africa ~75,000-10,000 ya. Spread across continents. Pre-historic cave art (~18,000 ya). Agriculture started (~10,000 ya). ~1400-1450cc brain.

## UNIT 8: Biology and Human Welfare

### Chapter: Human Health and Disease (Class 12)

*   **Health:** State of complete physical, mental, social well-being (WHO). Maintained by balanced diet, hygiene, exercise, awareness, vaccination, waste disposal. Affected by: Genetic disorders, Infections, Lifestyle.
*   **Disease:** Condition impairing normal functioning. Infectious (transmissible, caused by Pathogens) vs Non-infectious (non-transmissible, e.g., cancer, diabetes).
*   **Pathogens:** Bacteria, Viruses, Fungi, Protozoans, Helminths. Adaptations to survive in host.
*   **Common Infectious Diseases:**
    *   *Bacterial:* Typhoid (*Salmonella typhi*, contaminated food/water, high fever, weakness, intestinal perforation, Widal test). Pneumonia (*Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, infects lung alveoli-fill with fluid, fever, chills, cough, headache, droplet infection). Dysentery, Plague, Diphtheria.
    *   *Viral:* Common Cold (*Rhinoviruses*, infects nose/respiratory passage NOT lungs, droplet/fomite transmission). Influenza, Polio, Hepatitis, Dengue, Chikungunya, AIDS.
    *   *Protozoan:* Malaria (*Plasmodium* species - *P. vivax, P. malariae, P. falciparum*-malignant, requires 2 hosts: female *Anopheles* mosquito vector & human; multiplies in liver then RBCs, rupture of RBCs releases hemozoin toxin → chills/high fever cycles). Amoebiasis/Amoebic Dysentery (*Entamoeba histolytica*, contaminated food/water, housefly mechanical carrier, affects large intestine, bloody stools).
    *   *Helminthic:* Ascariasis (*Ascaris lumbricoides*-roundworm, contaminated soil/water/food, intestinal blockage). Filariasis/Elephantiasis (*Wuchereria bancrofti, W. malayi*-filarial worms, female *Culex/Aedes* mosquito vector, chronic inflammation of lymphatic vessels → gross swelling of limbs/genitals).
    *   *Fungal:* Ringworm (*Microsporum, Trichophyton, Epidermophyton*, affects skin/nails/scalp, dry scaly lesions, intense itching, transmitted via soil/contact/fomites).
*   **Prevention & Control:** Personal hygiene (hand washing, clean food/water). Public hygiene (waste/excreta disposal, vector control - eradicate breeding sites, mosquito nets, larvicidal fish-Gambusia). Vaccination, antibiotics/drugs.
*   **Immunity:** Ability to fight disease-causing organisms.
    *   **Innate Immunity:** Non-specific defence, present at birth. Barriers:
        *   *Physical:* Skin, Mucous coating (respiratory, GI, urogenital tracts).
        *   *Physiological:* Stomach acid, Saliva, Tears (lysozyme).
        *   *Cellular:* Phagocytes (Neutrophils/PMNL, Monocytes→Macrophages), Natural Killer (NK) cells.
        *   *Cytokine:* Interferons (proteins secreted by virus-infected cells, protect other cells from infection).
    *   **Acquired Immunity:** Pathogen-specific, develops after exposure, based on Memory. Mediated by Lymphocytes (B & T). Primary response (first encounter, slow, low intensity). Secondary/Anamnestic response (subsequent encounter, faster, high intensity, due to memory cells).
        *   *Humoral Immunity (Antibody-Mediated, AMI):* B-lymphocytes produce Antibodies (Immunoglobulins - protein army) against pathogens in body fluids. Structure: H2L2 (2 heavy, 2 light chains), variable region (antigen binding site). Types: IgA (colostrum, saliva, tears), IgM (pentamer, first response), IgG (most abundant, crosses placenta), IgE (allergic reactions), IgD.
        *   *Cell-Mediated Immunity (CMI):* Mediated by T-lymphocytes. Helper T-cells (activate B-cells, cytotoxic T-cells), Cytotoxic T-cells (kill infected cells/tumour cells), Suppressor T-cells, Memory T-cells. Responsible for Graft Rejection (distinguishes self/non-self). Immunosuppressants needed for transplants.
*   **Active vs Passive Immunity:**
    *   *Active:* Host produces own antibodies (after infection or vaccination). Slow, long-lasting memory.
    *   *Passive:* Ready-made antibodies given. Fast, short-lived, no memory. E.g., Colostrum (IgA from mother), Anti-tetanus serum, Anti-snake venom injection.
*   **Vaccination & Immunisation:** Principle: Memory of immune system. Preparation of antigenic proteins/inactivated/weakened pathogen (Vaccine) introduced → induces primary response & memory B/T cells → Secondary response upon actual infection is rapid/strong. Newer vaccines: Recombinant DNA tech (e.g., Hep B vaccine from yeast), DNA vaccines, Edible vaccines.
*   **Allergy:** Exaggerated immune response to certain environmental antigens (Allergens - dust, pollen, mites, dander). IgE antibodies involved. Mast cells release chemicals (Histamine, Serotonin) → inflammation. Symptoms: Sneezing, watery eyes, running nose, breathing difficulty, rash. Cause determination via exposure/tests. Treatment: Antihistamines, Adrenaline, Steroids. Modern lifestyle may lower immunity, increase allergy sensitivity.
*   **Autoimmunity:** Immune system attacks self-cells/molecules. Genetic/unknown reasons. E.g., Rheumatoid arthritis.
*   **Immune System in the Body:** Lymphoid organs, tissues, cells, antibodies. Lymphoid Organs: Primary (Bone marrow, Thymus - lymphocyte origin/maturation) & Secondary (Spleen, Lymph nodes, Tonsils, Peyer's patches of intestine, Appendix - sites for lymphocyte interaction with antigen, proliferation). MALT (Mucosal Associated Lymphoid Tissue) - lines major tracts, ~50% lymphoid tissue.
*   **AIDS (Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome):** Caused by HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus - a Retrovirus with RNA genome, reverse transcriptase). HIV attacks Helper T-lymphocytes (CD4 cells), macrophages. Reduces T-helper count drastically → severe immunodeficiency. Patient susceptible to opportunistic infections (bacteria like *Mycobacterium*, viruses, fungi, parasites like *Toxoplasma*) and cancers. Transmission: Sexual contact, contaminated blood/products, shared needles, infected mother to child (placenta). Not by touch/sharing. Incubation period: months to years. Diagnosis: ELISA (screening), Western Blot (confirmatory). Treatment: Anti-retroviral drugs (partially effective, prolong life, cannot cure). Prevention: Education, safe sex (condoms), screening blood, disposable needles, checking pregnant mothers (NACO, NGOs role).
*   **Cancer:** Uncontrolled cell growth & division. Loss of Contact Inhibition. Forms Tumors (masses of cells). Benign (localised, non-spreading) vs Malignant (neoplastic cells, grow rapidly, invade tissues - Metastasis - spread via blood/lymph). Causes (Carcinogens): Physical (Ionizing radiation - X-rays, gamma; Non-ionizing - UV), Chemical (Tobacco smoke - lung cancer), Biological (Oncogenic viruses, cellular oncogenes/proto-oncogenes activation). Detection & Diagnosis: Biopsy & Histopathology, Imaging (Radiography/X-ray, CT scan, MRI), Antibodies against cancer antigens, Molecular biology techniques. Treatment: Surgery, Radiotherapy (radiation), Chemotherapy (drugs - side effects), Immunotherapy (biological response modifiers like α-interferon).
*   **Drugs & Alcohol Abuse:** Taking drugs for non-medical purpose, impairing function. Adolescence is vulnerable period.
    *   *Opioids:* Bind opioid receptors (CNS, GI tract). Depressants, analgesics. Morphine (from poppy *Papaver somniferum* latex), Heroin/Smack (diacetylmorphine - more potent, obtained by acetylation of morphine). Taken by snorting/injection.
    *   *Cannabinoids:* Interact with cannabinoid receptors (mainly brain). Affect cardiovascular system. From *Cannabis sativa* inflorescences/leaves/resin. Marijuana, Hashish, Charas, Ganja. Taken by inhalation/ingestion. Some used medically.
    *   *Coca Alkaloids (Cocaine/Coke/Crack):* From *Erythroxylum coca* (South America). Interferes with dopamine transport. Potent CNS stimulant → euphoria, energy. Excessive dose → hallucinations.
    *   *Hallucinogens:* Alter thoughts, perceptions. LSD (from *Claviceps purpurea* fungus), Datura, Atropa belladonna.
    *   *Other Drugs:* Barbiturates, Amphetamines, Benzodiazepines (often misused).
    *   *Tobacco:* Smoked/chewed/snuffed. Contains Nicotine (alkaloid, stimulates adrenal gland → adrenaline release → ↑BP/HR). Associated with cancers (lung, oral, throat), bronchitis, emphysema, heart disease, ulcers. Smoking increases CO in blood, reduces HbO2.
*   **Addiction & Dependence:** Addiction (psychological attachment, compulsive use despite harm). Dependence (body shows withdrawal syndrome if drug/alcohol stopped abruptly - anxiety, nausea, sweating). Leads to social problems.
*   **Prevention & Control:** Avoid undue peer pressure, Education/counselling, Seek help from parents/peers, Look for danger signs, Seek professional/medical help (psychologists, de-addiction centres).

### Chapter: Microbes in Human Welfare (Class 12)

*   **Microbes:** Diverse (Bacteria, Fungi, Protozoa, Viruses, Viroids, Prions). Present everywhere. Useful & harmful.
*   **Microbes in Household Products:**
    *   *Curd:* *Lactobacillus* (Lactic Acid Bacteria, LAB) grow in milk, produce lactic acid → coagulates/digests milk proteins. Improves nutritional quality (↑Vitamin B12). Checks pathogens in stomach.
    *   *Dough Fermentation:* Bacteria produce CO2 (puffy appearance - Idli, Dosa). Yeast (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae* - Baker's yeast) used for bread making (CO2 causes dough to rise).
    *   *Toddy:* Traditional drink (S India) by fermenting palm sap.
    *   *Cheese:* Microbes used for fermentation & ripening. Swiss cheese (large holes due to CO2 by *Propionibacterium shermanii*). Roquefort cheese (ripened by *Penicillium roqueforti*).
*   **Microbes in Industrial Products:** Require growing microbes in large Fermentors.
    *   *Fermented Beverages:* Yeast (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae* - Brewer's yeast) ferments malted cereals/fruit juices → Ethanol. Wine, Beer (produced without distillation, lower alcohol %). Whisky, Brandy, Rum (produced by fermentation + distillation, higher alcohol %).
    *   *Antibiotics:* Chemicals produced by microbes killing/retarding other microbes. Penicillin (first antibiotic, discovered by Alexander Fleming from *Penicillium notatum*, working on *Staphylococcus*; Full potential established by Chain & Florey; Nobel Prize 1945). Used to treat diseases (plague, diphtheria, leprosy etc.).
    *   *Chemicals, Enzymes, Bioactive Molecules:*
        *   *Organic Acids:* *Aspergillus niger* (fungus) - Citric acid; *Acetobacter aceti* (bacterium) - Acetic acid; *Clostridium butylicum* (bacterium) - Butyric acid; *Lactobacillus* (bacterium) - Lactic acid.
        *   *Ethanol:* Yeast (*S. cerevisiae*).
        *   *Enzymes:* Lipases (detergents, remove oil stains), Pectinases & Proteases (clarify bottled juices), Streptokinase (from *Streptococcus*, modified by genetics, used as 'clot buster' for heart attack patients).
        *   *Bioactive Molecules:* Cyclosporin A (immunosuppressant in organ transplants, from fungus *Trichoderma polysporum*). Statins (blood cholesterol lowering agents, competitive inhibitor of synthesis enzyme, from yeast *Monascus purpureus*).
*   **Microbes in Sewage Treatment:** Municipal wastewater (sewage) contains human excreta, organic matter, microbes. Treatment in Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs) makes it less polluting before discharge.
    *   *Primary Treatment (Physical):* Removes floating debris (filtration/screening), grit/soil (sedimentation). Solids settle → Primary Sludge. Supernatant → Primary Effluent.
    *   *Secondary Treatment (Biological):* Primary effluent into large Aeration Tanks → agitated, air pumped → vigorous growth of aerobic microbes into Flocs (masses of bacteria + fungal filaments). Microbes consume organic matter → reduces Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD - measure of organic matter, O2 needed by bacteria to oxidise it). Higher BOD = more pollution potential. Effluent passed to Settling Tank → Flocs sediment (Activated Sludge). Small part of activated sludge used as inoculum back to aeration tank. Major part pumped to Anaerobic Sludge Digesters → Anaerobic bacteria digest bacteria/fungi/organic matter → produce Biogas (CH4, H2S, CO2) + digested sludge (manure). Treated effluent released into rivers/streams. Ministry of Environment & Forests initiated Ganga/Yamuna Action Plans.
*   **Microbes in Production of Biogas:** Biogas (mainly Methane CH4) produced by anaerobic digestion. Methanogens (anaerobic bacteria, e.g., *Methanobacterium*) grow on cellulosic material → produce CH4, CO2, H2. Found in anaerobic sludge, rumen of cattle (help digest cellulose). Cattle dung (gobar) rich in methanogens. Biogas Plant: Concrete tank (digester) fed with slurry (dung+water), floating cover collects gas, outlet for gas (fuel), outlet for spent slurry (fertilizer). Technology developed by IARI & KVIC.
*   **Microbes as Biocontrol Agents:** Using biological methods to control pests/diseases (reduces reliance on chemical pesticides/insecticides). Based on natural predation/parasitism.
    *   *Examples:* Ladybird beetle controls Aphids. Dragonflies control Mosquitoes. *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) bacteria controls butterfly/moth caterpillars (available as dried spores, sprayed; toxin gene cloned into plants - Bt cotton). *Trichoderma* fungus (free-living, in roots) controls plant pathogens. Baculoviruses (pathogens attacking insects/arthropods, genus *Nucleopolyhedrovirus*); species-specific, narrow spectrum, safe for non-targets; used in Integrated Pest Management (IPM).
*   **Microbes as Biofertilizers:** Organisms enriching soil nutrient quality (alternative to chemical fertilizers).
    *   *Bacteria:* *Rhizobium* (symbiotic with legume roots, forms nodules, fixes atmospheric N2). *Azospirillum*, *Azotobacter* (free-living in soil, fix N2).
    *   *Fungi:* Mycorrhiza (symbiotic association fungi + plant roots). Fungus absorbs Phosphorus from soil for plant, gets food from plant. E.g., *Glomus*. Plants with mycorrhiza show other benefits (stress tolerance, pathogen resistance).
    *   *Cyanobacteria (Blue-Green Algae):* Autotrophic, fix atmospheric N2. E.g., *Anabaena, Nostoc, Oscillatoria*. Important biofertilizers esp. in Paddy fields. Also add organic matter.
*   **Organic Farming:** Uses biofertilizers, biocontrol, minimal chemical use.

## UNIT 9: Biotechnology and Its Applications

### Chapter: Biotechnology - Principles and Processes (Class 12)

*   **Biotechnology:** Using biological systems/organisms/parts/molecular analogues for products/services useful to humans (EFB definition). Based on principles like Genetic Engineering & Bioprocess Engineering.
*   **Principles of Biotechnology:**
    *   *Genetic Engineering:* Techniques to alter chemistry of genetic material (DNA/RNA) → introduce into host → change host phenotype. Includes Recombinant DNA (rDNA) tech, Gene Cloning, Gene Transfer. Allows isolation/introduction of specific desired genes.
    *   *Bioprocess Engineering:* Maintaining sterile (microbe-free) conditions in chemical engineering processes → enable growth of only desired microbe/eukaryotic cell in large quantities for manufacturing products (antibiotics, vaccines, enzymes etc.).
*   **Tools of Recombinant DNA Technology:**
    *   **Restriction Enzymes ('Molecular Scissors'):** Endonucleases cutting DNA at specific Recognition Sequences (palindromic - read same forwards/backwards on opposite strands). Isolated from bacteria (defence mechanism). Naming convention (e.g., EcoRI from *E. coli* RY13). Produce sticky ends (overhangs) or blunt ends. Sticky ends facilitate ligation.
    *   **Cloning Vectors ('Vehicles'):** DNA molecule used to carry foreign DNA segment into host cell for replication/cloning. E.g., Plasmids (extrachromosomal circular DNA in bacteria), Bacteriophages (viruses infecting bacteria), BACs, YACs. Features required for cloning vector:
        *   *Origin of Replication (ori):* Sequence where replication starts; controls copy number.
        *   *Selectable Marker:* Gene helping select host cells containing the vector (transformants) from non-transformants (e.g., antibiotic resistance genes like ampR, tetR).
        *   *Cloning Sites (Recognition Sites):* Specific sites where restriction enzymes cut, allowing insertion of foreign DNA. Preferably single site for common enzymes, often located within a selectable marker (allows Insertional Inactivation for selecting recombinants). E.g., pBR322 vector.
    *   **Ligase Enzyme ('Molecular Glue'):** Joins DNA fragments (e.g., foreign DNA + vector DNA) by forming phosphodiester bonds. Requires sticky ends (usually).
    *   **Competent Host Organism:** Cell capable of taking up foreign/recombinant DNA (Transformation). Usually bacteria (*E. coli*), yeast, plant/animal cells. Made competent by treatment (CaCl2 + heat shock).
*   **Processes of Recombinant DNA Technology:**
    *   1.  **Isolation of Genetic Material (DNA):** Cell lysis (enzymes like lysozyme, cellulase, chitinase) → Removal of RNA (RNase), Proteins (Protease) → Precipitation of purified DNA by adding chilled ethanol.
    *   2.  **Cutting DNA at Specific Locations:** Using Restriction Enzymes (incubating purified DNA + enzyme at optimal conditions). Vector DNA also cut with same enzyme.
    *   3.  **Amplification of Gene of Interest using PCR:** Polymerase Chain Reaction amplifies specific DNA sequence exponentially. Requires template DNA, Primers (synthetic oligonucleotides complementary to region flanking target), Taq Polymerase (thermostable DNA Pol), dNTPs. Cycles: Denaturation (heat ~94°C, separate strands), Annealing (cool ~50-60°C, primers bind), Extension (heat ~72°C, Taq Pol synthesises new strands).
    *   4.  **Insertion of rDNA into Host Cell/Organism (Transformation):** Introducing ligation mixture (rDNA) into competent host cells. Methods: Heat shock, Microinjection (direct injection into animal cell nucleus), Biolistics/Gene Gun (DNA-coated gold/tungsten particles shot into plant cells), Disarmed Pathogen Vectors (*Agrobacterium tumefaciens* Ti plasmid for plants, Retroviruses for animals).
    *   5.  **Selection of Transformants & Recombinants:** Using selectable markers (e.g., antibiotic resistance, blue-white screening using insertional inactivation of lacZ gene coding β-galactosidase).
    *   6.  **Obtaining Foreign Gene Product (Recombinant Protein):** Cloning gene in expression vector → culture host cells → induce gene expression → desired protein produced. Large scale culture in Bioreactors.
    *   7.  **Downstream Processing:** Separation, purification, formulation of product from bioreactor culture. Quality control checks. Product ready for marketing.
*   **Bioreactors:** Large vessels (100-1000L) for biological conversion using microbes/plant/animal cells under controlled conditions. Provide optimal growth conditions (temp, pH, substrate, salts, vitamins, oxygen). Types: Simple Stirred-tank, Sparged Stirred-tank (bubbles increase O2 transfer). Ensure mixing, O2 availability.

### Chapter: Biotechnology and its Applications (Class 12)

*   **Biotechnology Applications:** In Therapeutics, Diagnostics, Genetically Modified (GM) crops for Agriculture, Processed food, Bioremediation, Waste treatment, Energy production. Critical research areas: Providing best catalyst (microbe/enzyme), Creating optimal conditions (engineering), Downstream processing.
*   **Biotechnological Applications in Agriculture:**
    *   *Need for GM Crops:* Increase yield from limited land, reduce post-harvest losses, increase crop tolerance (abiotic stress), reduce reliance on chemical pesticides (pest-resistance), enhance nutritional value.
    *   *Pest Resistant Plants:*
        *   *Bt Cotton:* Contains cry gene from *Bacillus thuringiensis* → produces insecticidal protein (Bt toxin). Toxin inactive in bacterium/plant, activated in alkaline gut of insect larva → binds gut wall, creates pores → swelling, lysis → death. Gene specific (e.g., cryIAc, cryIIAb control cotton bollworms; cryIAb controls corn borer). Reduces pesticide use. Bt Brinjal, Bt Corn etc. also developed.
        *   *Nematode Resistance (using RNA interference - RNAi):* RNAi is method of cellular defence in eukaryotes; involves silencing of specific mRNA due to complementary dsRNA → prevents translation. Strategy used against nematode *Meloidogyne incognita* infecting tobacco roots. Nematode-specific genes introduced into host plant (via *Agrobacterium* vector) → produce both sense & anti-sense RNA in host cells → form dsRNA → initiates RNAi → silences specific nematode mRNA → parasite cannot survive.
    *   *Other Applications:* Herbicide tolerant crops, Enhanced nutritional value (Golden Rice - Vitamin A enriched via β-carotene genes), increased shelf life.
*   **Biotechnological Applications in Medicine:**
    *   *Genetically Engineered Insulin:* rDNA tech allows large scale production. Human insulin gene (or sequences for A & B chains) introduced into *E. coli* / yeast. Earlier, insulin extracted from slaughtered cattle/pigs (caused allergy). Human insulin has A & B chains linked by disulfide bonds (proinsulin has extra C-peptide). Eli Lilly (1983) prepared A & B chains separately in *E. coli*, combined them → 'Humulin'.
    *   *Gene Therapy:* Collection of methods to correct gene defect. Correction of genetic disorder by delivering normal gene. First clinical therapy (1990) for Adenosine Deaminase (ADA) Deficiency (causes SCID - Severe Combined Immunodeficiency). Strategies: Enzyme replacement therapy (temporary), Bone marrow transplantation (temporary/risky), Gene therapy (introducing functional ADA cDNA via retroviral vector into patient's lymphocytes/stem cells cultured outside & returned - needs periodic infusion if lymphocytes used; potentially permanent if stem cells from marrow used early). Still experimental.
    *   *Molecular Diagnosis:* Early detection of disease/pathogen presence before symptoms appear. Conventional methods (serum/urine) detect late. rDNA tech, PCR, ELISA allow early diagnosis. PCR detects very low amounts of pathogen DNA/RNA (HIV, cancer gene mutations). rDNA probes detect specific gene sequences. ELISA (Enzyme Linked Immunosorbent Assay): Based on Antigen-Antibody interaction; detects presence of antigen (pathogen protein) or antibodies against pathogen (e.g., HIV detection).
*   **Transgenic Animals:** Animals with manipulated DNA (possess & express foreign gene). >95% are mice. Why created?
    *   *Normal Physiology & Development:* Study gene regulation, effects (e.g., insulin-like growth factor).
    *   *Study of Disease:* Models for human diseases (cancer, cystic fibrosis, rheumatoid arthritis, Alzheimer's) to study disease development, test treatments.
    *   *Biological Products:* Produce useful proteins/pharmaceuticals. α-1-antitrypsin (treats emphysema). Products for Phenylketonuria (PKU), cystic fibrosis. 'Rosie' cow (1997) produced human protein-enriched milk (human α-lactalbumin, 2.4 g/L).
    *   *Vaccine Safety Testing:* Mice used to test vaccine safety before human trials (e.g., polio vaccine).
    *   *Chemical Safety Testing (Toxicity):* Test toxicity of drugs/chemicals. More sensitive than non-transgenic animals.
*   **Ethical Issues:** Manipulation of living organisms raises ethical concerns. Indian Govt setup GEAC (Genetic Engineering Approval Committee) to make decisions regarding validity/safety of GM research & products for public use. Biopiracy (use of bio-resources/traditional knowledge by organisations without proper authorisation/compensation). Indian Patents Bill amended to address these. Need for regulations.

## UNIT 10: Ecology and Environment

### Chapter: Organisms and Populations (Class 12)

*   **Ecology:** Study of interactions among organisms, and between organisms & their physical (abiotic) environment. Levels: Organism → Population → Community → Ecosystem → Biome → Biosphere. Ecology at organism level = Physiological ecology (adaptation to environment).
*   **Organism and Its Environment:** Rotation & tilt of Earth cause seasons, variation in temperature/precipitation → major Biomes formation (Desert, Rainforest, Grassland, Tundra, etc.). Regional/local variations → diverse Habitats. Habitat components: Biotic (pathogens, parasites, predators, competitors) & Abiotic (Temperature, Water, Light, Soil). Niche (functional role/position of species in ecosystem, includes resources utilized, tolerance range).
    *   **Major Abiotic Factors:**
        *   *Temperature:* Most relevant factor. Affects enzyme kinetics, BMR. Eurythermal (tolerate wide range) vs Stenothermal (narrow range).
        *   *Water:* Life originated in water. Availability crucial (esp. deserts). Affects productivity/distribution. Salinity affects aquatic organisms. Euryhaline (wide tolerance) vs Stenohaline (narrow).
        *   *Light:* Source of energy (photosynthesis). Quality, Intensity, Duration (Photoperiod - affects flowering, migration, foraging) important. UV harmful.
        *   *Soil:* Nature/properties depend on climate, weathering process, development method. Affects vegetation type. Properties: composition, grain size, aggregation, pH, mineral composition, topography, water holding capacity.
    *   **Responses to Abiotic Factors:** Organisms maintain constant internal environment (Homeostasis) despite external changes, or conform.
        *   *Regulate:* Maintain constant body temp (Thermoregulation - birds, mammals) & osmotic concentration (Osmoregulation). Energetically expensive. Benefit: constant performance.
        *   *Conform:* Internal environment changes with ambient conditions (most plants, animals). Energetically 'cheap'. Performance varies.
        *   *Migrate:* Temporary movement away from stressful habitat to more favourable area, return when conditions improve (e.g., birds - Keoladeo National Park).
        *   *Suspend:* Reduce metabolic activity during unfavourable conditions. Hibernation (winter sleep - bears, squirrels), Aestivation (summer sleep - snails, fish), Diapause (suspended development stage - zooplankton, insects). Spores (bacteria, fungi), Seeds (plants).
*   **Adaptations:** Attribute (morphological, physiological, behavioural) enabling organism to survive & reproduce in its habitat; evolved via natural selection.
    *   *Examples:* Kangaroo rat (internal fat oxidation for water, concentrated urine). Desert plants (thick cuticle, sunken stomata, CAM pathway, reduced leaves/spines). Cold climate mammals (Allen's Rule: shorter ears/limbs to minimise heat loss; Blubber). High altitude humans (↑RBC production, ↓Hb affinity, ↑breathing rate). Archaebacteria (tolerate extremes). Desert lizards (bask/seek shade - behavioural thermoregulation). Fish (Antifreeze proteins).
*   **Populations:** Group of individuals of same species in a given area, sharing/competing for resources, interbreeding.
    *   **Population Attributes:** Characteristics of group, not individual. Birth Rate (Natality), Death Rate (Mortality), Sex Ratio, Age Distribution (represented by Age Pyramids: Pre-reproductive, Reproductive, Post-reproductive phases; Types: Expanding, Stable, Declining). Population Size/Density (N; number per unit area/volume).
    *   **Population Growth:** Size changes depending on: Natality (+), Mortality (-), Immigration (+), Emigration (-). Growth equation: Nt+1 = Nt + [(B+I) – (D+E)]. Change in density (dN/dt).
        *   *Exponential Growth (Geometric):* Resources unlimited. J-shaped curve. dN/dt = rN. r = intrinsic rate of natural increase (b-d). Integral form: Nt = N0 * e^(rt). Potential for huge increase.
        *   *Logistic Growth (Verhulst-Pearl):* Resources limited. Carrying Capacity (K) = max population size environment can sustain. Growth slows as N approaches K. S-shaped (Sigmoid) curve. dN/dt = rN * [(K-N)/K]. Most realistic model.
    *   **Life History Variation:** Organisms evolve efficient reproductive strategy ('Darwinian fitness'). Variations in breeding frequency (Semelparity - breed once, e.g., Pacific salmon, bamboo; Iteroparity - breed multiple times, e.g., birds, mammals), number/size of offspring. Trade-offs exist.
*   **Population Interactions:** Interspecific interactions between species in a community. (+ = benefit, - = harm, 0 = neutral).
    *   *Predation (+/-):* Predator kills & eats Prey. Role: energy transfer, control prey population, maintain species diversity (reduce competition). Prey defences: camouflage, warning colouration, mimicry (Batesian, Mullerian), thorns, toxins.
    *   *Competition (-/-):* Both species negatively affected. Occurs when resources are limited. Intraspecific (within species) vs Interspecific (between species). Gause's Competitive Exclusion Principle (two species competing for same limited resource cannot coexist indefinitely if niches identical). Coexistence possible via Resource Partitioning (using resource differently - time/place/part). Connell's field experiment (barnacles).
    *   *Parasitism (+/-):* Parasite depends on Host for food/shelter, harms host. Ectoparasites (on surface - lice, ticks, Cuscuta plant) vs Endoparasites (inside body - tapeworm, Ascaris, Plasmodium; complex life cycles, reduced sensory/digestive systems, high reproduction). Brood Parasitism (Cuckoo lays eggs in Crow's nest).
    *   *Commensalism (+/0):* One benefits, other unaffected. Orchid on mango branch, Barnacles on whale, Cattle Egret & grazing cattle, Clown fish & Sea anemone.
    *   *Mutualism (+/+):* Both partners benefit (Symbiosis). Lichens (Fungus + Algae/Cyanobacteria), Mycorrhizae (Fungi + Plant roots), Pollination (Plant + Pollinator - co-evolution, rewards), Fig & Fig Wasp (specific pollinators, oviposition site).

### Chapter: Ecosystem (Class 12)

*   **Ecosystem:** Functional unit of nature; interaction between Biotic (living) & Abiotic (non-living) components. Vary in size (pond to forest). Terrestrial vs Aquatic. Structure & Function are key aspects.
*   **Ecosystem - Structure and Function:** Interaction of biotic/abiotic components results in physical structure characteristic for each ecosystem type. Vertical distribution of species = Stratification (e.g., layers in forest). Functional components:
    *   **1. Productivity:** Rate of biomass production (amount of biomass per unit area per unit time; g/m²/yr or kcal/m²/yr).
        *   *Primary Productivity:* Rate of biomass production by Producers (photosynthesis). Depends on plant species, photosynthesis ability, nutrient availability, environmental factors. Highest in tropics, oceans have low productivity (except estuaries, coral reefs). Gross Primary Productivity (GPP) = Total photosynthesis rate. Net Primary Productivity (NPP) = GPP - Respiration loss (R). NPP is the biomass available for consumers.
        *   *Secondary Productivity:* Rate of new organic matter formation by Consumers.
    *   **2. Decomposition:** Breakdown of complex organic matter (Detritus - dead remains) into inorganic substances (CO2, H2O, nutrients) by Decomposers (microbes - bacteria, fungi). Essential for nutrient recycling. Steps:
        *   *Fragmentation:* Detritivores (earthworm etc.) break down detritus → increases surface area.
        *   *Leaching:* Water-soluble inorganic nutrients go down into soil horizon.
        *   *Catabolism:* Bacterial/fungal enzymes degrade detritus into simpler inorganic substances.
        *   *Humification:* Accumulation of dark, amorphous, colloid-rich substance (Humus). Highly resistant to microbes, slow decomposition, reservoir of nutrients.
        *   *Mineralisation:* Humus further degraded by microbes → release of inorganic nutrients.
        *   *Factors affecting:* Oxygen (aerobic faster), Chemical composition of detritus (lignin/chitin slow, N/sugar rich fast), Climatic factors (Warm, moist environment favours; low temp/anaerobiosis inhibit).
    *   **3. Energy Flow:** Sun is primary source (except deep sea vents). Producers capture solar energy (Photosynthetically Active Radiation - PAR, only 2-10% PAR captured). Energy flows unidirectionally: Producer → Herbivore (Primary consumer) → Carnivore (Secondary/Tertiary consumer). Follows Laws of Thermodynamics (Energy neither created/destroyed; Energy lost as heat at each transfer).
        *   *Food Chain:* Linear sequence of eating/being eaten. Grazing Food Chain (GFC - starts with producers) vs Detritus Food Chain (DFC - starts with dead organic matter/detritus; major energy flow conduit in many ecosystems). Chains interconnected → Food Web (more realistic, stable).
        *   *Trophic Levels:* Position in food chain (Producer T1, Primary consumer T2, Secondary T3, Tertiary T4). Organism can occupy >1 trophic level. Standing Crop = mass of living material at a trophic level at particular time.
        *   *Ecological Efficiency (10% Law - Lindeman):* Only ~10% energy transferred from one trophic level to the next. Large fraction lost as heat (respiration), undigested/uneaten. Limits number of trophic levels.
    *   **4. Nutrient Cycling (Biogeochemical Cycles):** Movement of nutrient elements through various components (biotic/abiotic) of ecosystem. Standing State = amount of nutrients in soil at given time. Types:
        *   *Gaseous Cycles:* Reservoir in atmosphere (e.g., Nitrogen Cycle, Carbon Cycle).
        *   *Sedimentary Cycles:* Reservoir in Earth's crust/rocks (e.g., Phosphorus Cycle, Sulphur Cycle).
        *   *Carbon Cycle:* Reservoir (atmosphere, oceans, rocks, fossil fuels). Cycling via photosynthesis (uptake), respiration (release), decomposition, combustion, volcanoes. Human impact (fossil fuel burning, deforestation) increases atmospheric CO2 → global warming.
        *   *Phosphorus Cycle:* Reservoir (rocks - phosphate). Weathering releases phosphate → soil → uptake by plants → food chain → decomposition releases P back. No significant respiratory release. Input from weathering slow. Human impact (fertilizers, detergents).
*   **Ecological Pyramids:** Graphical representation of relationship between trophic levels (number, biomass, energy). Base = Producer (T1), Apex = Top consumer.
    *   *Pyramid of Numbers:* Based on number of individuals per trophic level. Usually upright (producers > herbivores > carnivores). Can be inverted (Tree ecosystem: 1 producer supports many herbivores/parasites).
    *   *Pyramid of Biomass:* Based on total dry weight (biomass) at each level. Usually upright (terrestrial). Can be inverted (Aquatic ecosystem: Phytoplankton biomass < Zooplankton biomass).
    *   *Pyramid of Energy:* Based on energy flow rate (productivity) at successive levels. Always upright (energy decreases at each transfer due to 10% law/heat loss). Most accurate representation.
*   **Ecological Succession:** Gradual, fairly predictable change in species composition of an area over time, leading towards a Climax community (stable, near equilibrium with environment). Entire sequence = Sere; individual stages = Seral stages/communities.
    *   *Types:* Primary Succession (starts on bare area with no prior life - rock, lava, new pond; very slow). Secondary Succession (starts on area where life existed but was destroyed, soil present - abandoned field, burnt forest; faster). Hydrarch Succession (starts in water → progresses to mesic/land). Xerarch Succession (starts on dry land → progresses to mesic).
    *   *Process:* Pioneer Species (first invaders, hardy, e.g., lichens on rock, phytoplankton in water) modify environment → allows subsequent species (seral communities) to invade/replace → gradual increase in diversity, biomass, humus, niche specialisation → reaches stable Climax Community. Changes are directional.
*   **Ecosystem Services:** Products/benefits obtained from ecosystems. Examples: Forest (purify air, mitigate drought/flood, nutrient cycling, habitat, biodiversity, timber, fuel, aesthetic value). Pollination (bees, birds). Carbon fixation. Oxygen production. Soil formation. Valued by Robert Costanza et al. (~US$ 33 trillion/year). Need to conserve ecosystems to maintain these services.

### Chapter: Biodiversity and Conservation (Class 12)

*   **Biodiversity (Biological Diversity):** Variability among living organisms from all sources (terrestrial, marine, aquatic ecosystems & ecological complexes). Includes diversity within species, between species, and of ecosystems. Term popularised by Edward Wilson.
*   **Levels of Biodiversity:**
    *   *Genetic Diversity:* Diversity of genes within a species. High diversity ensures adaptability. E.g., *Rauwolfia vomitoria* (Himalayas - variation in reserpine potency/concentration), Rice varieties in India (>50,000), Mango varieties (>1,000).
    *   *Species Diversity:* Variety of species within a region. Species Richness (number of species) vs Species Evenness (relative abundance). Western Ghats > Eastern Ghats amphibian diversity.
    *   *Ecological Diversity:* Variety of ecosystems (habitats, communities) in a region. E.g., India (deserts, rainforests, mangroves, coral reefs, alpine meadows) > Norway.
*   **Magnitude of Biodiversity:** Total species on Earth estimated (Robert May) ~7 million (more conservative estimates exist). Currently described species ~1.5 million. Animals >70%, Plants ~22%. Insects most species-rich animal group (>70% animals). Fungi species > combined total of fish, amphibians, reptiles, mammals. Prokaryote diversity largely unknown. India has ~2.4% world's land area, but ~8.1% global species diversity (one of 12 mega-diversity countries).
*   **Patterns of Biodiversity:**
    *   *Latitudinal Gradients:* Species diversity decreases from equator towards poles. Tropics (latitudes 23.5°N to 23.5°S) have highest diversity. Reasons: Less seasonal variation (predictable environment), more solar energy (higher productivity), longer evolutionary time (undisturbed by glaciations) → greater niche specialisation & species richness.
    *   *Species-Area Relationships:* Alexander von Humboldt observed: Within a region, species richness increases with increasing explored area, but only up to a limit. Relationship on log scale is straight line: log S = log C + Z log A (S=richness, A=area, Z=slope/regression coefficient, C=Y-intercept). Z value generally 0.1-0.2 regardless of group/region. For very large areas (continents), Z value steeper (0.6-1.2).
*   **Importance of Species Diversity to Ecosystem:** David Tilman's experiments: Plots with more species showed less year-to-year variation in biomass (more stable productivity), higher productivity, increased resistance to disturbances (e.g., drought, invasion). Rich biodiversity essential for ecosystem health & survival of human race ('Rivet popper hypothesis' - Paul Ehrlich: ecosystem=airplane, species=rivets, loss of key species/rivets → weakens ecosystem/flight safety).
*   **Loss of Biodiversity:** Current extinction rates estimated 100-1000 times faster than pre-human rates → Sixth Extinction (anthropogenic). IUCN Red List (2004) documents >15,500 threatened species. Recent extinctions (Dodo, Quagga, Thylacine, Steller's Sea Cow, 3 tiger subspecies). Amphibians more vulnerable. Consequences: Decline in plant production, lowered resistance to perturbations, increased variability in processes (productivity, water use, pest/disease cycles).
    *   **Causes ('The Evil Quartet'):**
        *   1.  *Habitat Loss and Fragmentation:* Most important cause. Due to deforestation, urbanization, agriculture. Amazon rainforest ('lungs of planet') being cleared. Fragmentation degrades habitats.
        *   2.  *Over-exploitation:* Harvesting resources unsustainably (e.g., Steller's sea cow, Passenger pigeon extinction). Overfishing threatens marine populations.
        *   3.  *Alien Species Invasions:* Introduced species turn invasive, cause decline/extinction of native species (lack natural predators). E.g., Nile Perch in Lake Victoria (cichlid extinction), Parthenium/Lantana/Eichhornia (invasive weeds), African Catfish (*Clarias gariepinus*) threat to native catfish.
        *   4.  *Co-extinctions:* Extinction of one species leads to extinction of associated species (e.g., host & parasite, plant & pollinator).
*   **Biodiversity Conservation:** Why conserve?
    *   *Narrowly Utilitarian:* Direct economic benefits from nature - Food, Firewood, Fibre, Construction material, Industrial products (tannins, dyes, resins, perfumes), Medicines (derived from >25% drugs, ~25,000 plant species). Bioprospecting (exploring molecular, genetic, species-level diversity for economic products).
    *   *Broadly Utilitarian:* Ecosystem Services - Oxygen production, Pollination, Climate regulation, Flood/erosion control, Nutrient cycling, Aesthetic pleasure.
    *   *Ethical Arguments:* Intrinsic value of every species. Moral duty to protect life forms for future generations.
*   **How to Conserve Biodiversity?**
    *   **In situ Conservation (On-site):** Protecting species in their natural habitat. Protects entire ecosystem.
        *   *Biodiversity Hotspots:* Regions with very high species richness, high endemism (species confined to region), and under threat. Identified globally (36 hotspots). India has 4: Western Ghats & Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma, Himalaya, Sundaland. Strict protection reduces extinction rate.
        *   *Protected Areas:* National Parks (strict protection, no human activity), Wildlife Sanctuaries (protection to animals, limited human activity allowed), Biosphere Reserves (large areas, zoned: Core-undisturbed, Buffer-limited human activity/research, Transition-human settlements/sustainable use). India: 104 NPs, 551 WLSs, 18 BRs.
        *   *Sacred Groves:* Forest tracts protected by communities based on religious/cultural traditions (Meghalaya, Western Ghats, Rajasthan, MP). Refuge for rare species.
    *   **Ex situ Conservation (Off-site):** Protecting threatened species outside their natural habitat.
        *   *Methods:* Zoological Parks (Zoos), Botanical Gardens, Wildlife Safari Parks, Cryopreservation (preserving gametes/embryos/tissues at very low temp -196°C in liquid N2), Seed Banks (preserving seeds viable for long periods), Gene Banks, Tissue Culture. Captive breeding programs.
*   **Conservation Efforts:** International (Earth Summit - Rio 1992, Convention on Biological Diversity - CBD; World Summit - Johannesburg 2002) & National efforts are needed.
